AI image to video generator vs slideshow: which do you need?

An AI image to video generator animates one still into new motion; a photo slideshow narrates many real photos. Compare accuracy, cost and best uses.

The short answer

An AI image to video generator takes one still and invents a few seconds of new motion from it: a camera move, a turning subject, sometimes sound. A photo slideshow arranges many real photos into a longer narrated video that shows only what was photographed. Use the first for mood shots and ad hooks, the second for listings and products. Videngine makes the second kind, from a page, a listing or a row of data.

Two jobs that share one search

Image-to-video AI and a photo slideshow both start from pictures, but one makes new footage and the other presents the photos you already have.

A search for an AI video maker from photo can lead to either, and picking the wrong one costs time at best and a misleading video at worst.

Image-to-video is generative: a model takes one still and a prompt describing the motion, and predicts new frames that were never photographed. A slideshow is arrangement: real photos play in order with a gentle pan or zoom, captions, title cards and usually a voiceover. Nothing new is drawn.

What an AI image to video generator does

An AI image to video generator treats your photo as the starting point and generates the motion, so the result is new footage rather than your picture moving.

Runway's help centre puts the split neatly: for image to video, the prompt should describe the desired motion, and you do not need to describe the contents of the image. Its most advanced model, Gen-4.5, supports both text to video and image to video.

Other tools, from their own pages:

  • Google Veo: Google DeepMind's Veo page introduces Veo 3.1 for text-to-video and image-to-video, guided by reference images or by first and last frames. It adds sound effects, ambient noise and dialogue, outputs 1080p and 4K, and says Veo videos are 8 seconds long and can be extended. If you were searching for Imagen video AI, this is the Google model to look at.
  • Kling: Kling's Video 3.0 page offers to animate any image and produces up to 15 seconds in a single generation, with multi-shot sequences and native audio.
  • Meta AI: for Meta AI image to video, Meta's help centre covers editing and animating images with Meta AI and Vibes, with the image uploaded in your prompt.

What they share matters more: clips are seconds long, each generation comes out differently, and any charge is usually per generation, kept or not. For the wider field, see the best AI video generators by what you need to make.

The accuracy risk for listings and products

Generated motion can show things that are not there, which is a problem whenever the viewer is deciding on a real home, vehicle, boat or product.

A model animating a photo has to fill in whatever the photo does not show. A camera drifting past a doorway needs a room nobody photographed. A car that turns needs a side that was never shot. A product that opens or folds does what the model expects, not what the product does. Text, badges and number plates can change between frames.

That is not a fault in one tool; it is what generation means. For a listing, a wrong frame lands on the buyer first and on you second: a viewing booked for a view that does not exist, a test drive for trim the car lacks. Treat any generated clip as an illustration, never as evidence: label it, check every frame, and check your market's advertising rules. When to use each, and when to use both

Use a slideshow when the viewer is judging the real thing, image-to-video when you need atmosphere or a shot you never filmed, and both when an ad needs a moving opener but the facts must stay true.

JobBetter fitWhy
Property listingSlideshowBuyers judge the real rooms; generated motion can invent space
Used vehicle listingSlideshowTrim, condition and colour must match the vehicle for sale
Product catalogue, many SKUsSlideshowOne row per SKU, one template, the same result each time
Hotel or venue mood openerImage-to-video, labelledSets atmosphere without promising a specific detail
Social ad openerBothA generated clip catches attention; real photos carry the offer

To use both, make the clip in the generator, check it, then supply it as one of your own assets alongside the real photos. Put it where nobody will mistake it for evidence, such as an opening mood shot, with a card saying it is illustrative. Let the photos carry the facts: rooms, condition, colour, price.

A decision checklist

Answer these in order; the first clear yes usually decides it.

  1. Is the viewer deciding on a specific real home, vehicle or SKU? Slideshow, with the real photos.
  2. Would a wrong detail cost you a complaint or a refund? Slideshow.
  3. Do you need tens or hundreds of videos from similar sources? A slideshow from a template and your data.
  4. Will you remake the same video next month with a new price? A template; a generation comes out differently each time.
  5. Does it need a voice explaining details, with captions? A narrated slideshow.
  6. Do you need motion that was never filmed? Image-to-video, or film it.
  7. Is it a mood shot where nothing specific is promised? Image-to-video, labelled and checked.

How Videngine does the slideshow side

Videngine turns a page, a listing or a row of data into a narrated photo video using the source's own photos, and it never animates a still into new motion.

It works from a URL (reading the page's photos), a CSV or JSON row with photo URLs, or a sitemap to pick many pages. One source gives one video, usually a 9:16 and a 16:9 cut (1:1 and 4:5 too), up to 1080p, as MP4 or WebM.

When a site serves resized copies, Videngine asks for the original first. Photos below 1,920 px on the long side are enlarged with a Lanczos filter and a light sharpen, never more than 3×. Each is graded by its original long side (sharp from 1,600 px, ok from 1,000 px, low below that), the studio flags the soft ones, and every photo is turned the right way up.

Each photo gets a slow zoom and a short fade in, a restrained Ken Burns effect. A photo that does not fit the frame sits over a blurred fill of itself, so landscape listing photos work in 9:16 without cropping the subject out. A script is written from the source and read by a house voice, another synthetic voice or your own audio; captions are transcribed from the finished audio. Brand type, colours, logo, title cards and an optional quiet music bed complete it.

Every video is reviewed in the studio before it goes out. Deliver by download, to a Google Drive folder you own, to a connected YouTube channel, or into the source page on a GitHub-connected static site. When Videngine is the wrong tool

If you need motion that was never filmed, Videngine cannot make it; use an image-to-video tool such as Veo, Runway or Kling, or film the shot.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between an image to video generator and a slideshow maker?

An image to video generator uses AI to create new motion from one still, so parts of the clip were never photographed. A slideshow maker arranges many real photos with pan or zoom, captions and often narration, and shows only what the camera captured.

Can I use an AI video generator from an image for a property or car listing?

You can, but it is risky for anything a buyer relies on, because generated motion can show rooms, views or trim that are not there. Use the real photos for the listing and keep any generated clip as a labelled mood shot.

How do I convert a photo to video without AI inventing anything?

Make a slideshow: the photo stays as it is and moves slowly in the frame with a gentle zoom or pan. Add captions and a voiceover to explain it. Videngine's photo scenes work this way, using a slow zoom.

Can I mix an image to video AI clip with real photos?

Yes. Generate and check the clip first, then add it as an asset alongside the real photos. Put it where it reads as atmosphere, such as an opening shot, and label it so nobody takes it as evidence.

Does Videngine animate still photos?

No. Videngine gives each photo a slow zoom and a short fade in, and never generates new motion from a still. If you need a shot that was never filmed, use an image-to-video tool and supply the result as an asset.
