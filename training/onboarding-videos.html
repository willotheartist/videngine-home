Onboarding videos: for new staff and new customers

Make onboarding videos for new staff and new customers from the documents you already keep: a series plan, what to leave out, delivery and keeping it current.

The short answer

Onboarding videos work best as a short series of one- to two-minute videos, each made from a document the business already keeps: the staff handbook for new starters, the getting-started help articles for new customers. Leave out anything that changes weekly and any personal data. Videngine turns those pages into narrated, captioned MP4s in your brand, reviewed before release and re-rendered when the document changes.

Two onboarding series from documents you already keep

One method serves both audiences: one short video for each question a newcomer asks, made from the page that already answers it.

Those answers usually exist already, in a staff handbook, an IT setup page, a help centre or a welcome email. The training videos hub covers the wider method; this page applies it to onboarding.

What not to put in onboarding videos

Keep anything that changes weekly, and anything about an identifiable person, out of the video; put it in the email or document the video sits beside.

The video should carry the stable how and why; text carries the who, when and how much.

  • Weekly changes: rotas, this week’s timetable, meeting dates, current offers and prices.
  • Personal data: a new starter’s name, start date, salary or employee number; a customer’s account name or records in a screenshot. The UK ICO’s guide to personal information defines personal data as information that relates to an identified or identifiable individual, and lists a name, an identification number, location data and an online identifier among the identifiers. Say “your manager”, not a name, and use a demo account for screenshots.
  • Secrets: passwords, door codes and Wi-Fi keys. A video can be forwarded and kept long after the code changes.
  • Wording people must accept: contract terms and signed policies. The video explains what a policy is for; the document stays the record.

A quick test: if a line would be wrong in three months, it belongs in the email.

Keeping an onboarding series current

Tie every video to one source document and one owner, and re-render the video when that document changes rather than patching the old file.

Keep a short register of video, source, owner, version and date checked, and review it whenever a policy, tool or product changes.

In Videngine a video ledger records versions, and a rebuild is saved as a new version, for example _v2, beside the original rather than overwriting it. A changed policy line is a script edit; a new logo is one brand-token change; a mispronounced name is one lexicon entry. Captions are transcribed again from the new narration, and re-rendering the same video in the same month is free.

Keep the links in one place, such as the onboarding email template or the LMS course, so a new version means one edit.

A build plan for a first onboarding series

Start from the questions newcomers actually ask, not from the documents, and finish one audience’s series before starting the other.

  1. Collect the questions. Ask recent starters and managers what they asked in the first fortnight; for customers, read first-month support tickets.
  2. Map each question to a document. Where no page answers it, write the page first.
  3. Strip what does not belong. Move dates, names, prices and codes into the welcome email.
  4. Cut to four to six videos. One question each, under two minutes, in the order a newcomer needs them.
  5. Set the brand once. Typefaces, colours, logo, voice and a lexicon of product, team and office names.
  6. Make one video and review it with the document owner. Edit the script, or supply approved wording word for word.
  7. Render the rest as a batch. Review each; render 16:9 for the LMS and 9:16 for phones.
  8. Deliver and register. Record each video’s source, owner and version, and set the next check date.

When a recorded welcome from a real person is better

When the point of the video is the person, record the person: a founder’s welcome, a manager’s hello before day one, or an account manager introducing themselves to a new customer.

Those welcome videos work because the viewer sees a face they are about to meet; a minute on a phone or webcam, sent personally, does that better than any template. A team that hires a few people a year may need only a good handbook and a named buddy.

Videngine is also the wrong tool if you want an on-screen presenter (an avatar tool fits; Videngine vs Synthesia compares one), SCORM packages, quizzes or completion records (use e-learning authoring software and an LMS), narration in other languages, or hands-on onboarding where a live walk-through works better.

The two mix well: record the welcome yourself, or upload your own audio as narration, and let the documents carry the rest.

Onboarding video questions

What should employee onboarding videos cover?

Cover what every new starter asks in the first two weeks: how the first week runs, how the team works, which tools they need, how leave and expenses work, and where to get help. Make one short video per question from the handbook page that already answers it.

How long should onboarding videos be?

Aim for 60 seconds to two minutes, with one question per video. Short videos are easier to watch in a busy first week and easier to update when one document changes.

Can you make customer onboarding videos from help articles?

Yes. Getting-started articles are already written as steps and kept current by the support team. Videngine reads an article by URL, writes a script you can edit and builds scenes over the article's own screenshots.

Can Videngine track who has watched our onboarding videos?

No. Videngine makes MP4 and WebM files and delivers them as downloads, to a Google Drive folder you own, to YouTube or to a static website page. It does not track learners, make SCORM packages or add quizzes.
