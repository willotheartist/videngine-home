Videngine or InVideo?

Videngine is an InVideo alternative when your videos already have a source: a product page, a listing, an article or a row of data, with its own photos. InVideo AI is the better choice when you start from an idea, because it writes the script and fills the screen with stock or AI-generated footage. Videngine renders hundreds of narrated, captioned videos in your brand from material you already have, and you review each one before it goes out.

Deciding quickly. If the video starts from an idea, use InVideo. If it starts from a page, a listing or a spreadsheet you already have, and there are dozens or hundreds of them, use Videngine.

What each one is

InVideo is an AI video generator and browser editor for creators; Videngine is a rendering engine for videos that repeat across a catalogue, a site or a dataset.

InVideo

InVideo’s main product is invideo AI: you describe a video and it writes the script, adds visuals, an AI voiceover, subtitles and music. Its AI video generator page lists more than 16 million stock photos and videos, showing iStock, Storyblocks and Shutterstock as sources, and voiceovers in over 50 languages. You then change the result by typing commands, such as changing the accent or deleting a scene.

Its homepage now calls it “the agentic video editor for serious creatives”: a multitrack timeline in the browser where an AI agent edits your footage from plain-language instructions, and where you can generate new shots with models such as Veo 3.1, Sora 2, Kling and Seedance. It also offers a library of AI actors, an avatar of yourself made from a 60-second video, voice cloning from a 30-second recording, and an MCP connection so an assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT can create and edit videos in its editor. Its intro maker is the same prompt flow aimed at intro videos, and invideo Studio, a template editor with thousands of templates, is offered alongside it.

Billing is per seat, plus credits. Credits pay for AI work, including agent processing and generating images, video, voice and music, and the amount depends on the models used and the work done. Manual timeline editing and exporting don’t use credits, and unused plan credits don’t roll over. The help centre describes a free plan with limited credits that reset weekly.

Videngine

Videngine is a programmatic video engine. Give it a URL, a sitemap, a CSV or a JSON payload and it reads each page, product, listing or article in a real browser, writes a script you can accept or edit, records the narration and renders the source’s own photos into scene templates written as code, with your typefaces, colours and logo set once. Captions are transcribed from the finished audio, so they match what was said.

Every video is reviewed in the studio, then downloaded, sent to a Google Drive folder you own, published to a connected YouTube channel, or committed to your website with a player, a transcript and VideoObject markup. There are no avatars, no stock library and no generated footage. Videngine is launching soon; plans start at $79 a month for 50 finished videos.

What 250 videos a month looks like

At 250 videos a month, InVideo is 250 separate projects drawn against a credit balance, and Videngine is a batch run, a review queue and a fixed monthly price.

Take a retailer adding a one-minute video to 250 product pages, or a publisher turning 250 articles into Shorts and long-form cuts. Here’s the work and the bill on each.

With InVideo

  1. Brief each video. Paste a prompt, a script or the page’s text for each product or article, and upload its photos if you want them used instead of stock.
  2. Let the agent build it. It writes the script and picks stock or generates visuals; generated clips and voice draw on credits.
  3. Edit by command or by hand. Check each script, swap scenes and fix any names or figures that came out wrong, one project at a time.
  4. Export and upload. Download each MP4 and post it wherever it needs to go.

With Videngine

  1. Pick the sources. Choose 250 pages from your sitemap, or upload a CSV or JSON file with one row per video.
  2. Run the batch. Each page or row becomes a script, a narration and a render in your template, unattended.
  3. Review in the studio. Approve, edit a line or rebuild; a rebuild is saved as a new version beside the original.
  4. Deliver. Download, send to Google Drive, queue to YouTube or publish into the page the video came from.

When InVideo is better, and when to choose an InVideo alternative

InVideo is the better tool for content creators, faceless explainer channels and anyone with an idea but no footage; Videngine is better when the material already exists and the same kind of video has to be made again and again.

Videngine is the wrong tool for the first job. It has no stock library, doesn’t generate footage, has no presenters and narrates in English only. If you’re weighing tools that do invent the pictures, our guides to text-to-video AI and the best AI video generators cover that ground.

Choose InVideo if

  • You start from an idea or a script, not existing pages or photos
  • You run a faceless YouTube or Shorts channel and need visuals you don’t have
  • You want generated footage, AI actors or an avatar of yourself
  • You need voiceovers in languages other than English
  • You want a timeline editor with an AI agent for your own footage

Choose Videngine if

  • Every video comes from a page, listing, product or data row you already have
  • The video must show the real item, from its own photos
  • Dozens or hundreds of videos need the same branded format
  • Names and figures must be stated and pronounced correctly every time
  • You want a price per finished video and publishing to YouTube or your own site

Questions

Is Videngine an InVideo alternative?

For videos made from material you already have, yes: product pages, listings, articles and data rows, rendered in batches with your own photos and brand. For videos that start from an idea and need stock or generated footage, InVideo is built for that job and Videngine isn’t.

Can InVideo AI make videos in bulk from a spreadsheet?

We didn’t find a CSV import, a bulk feature or a public API on InVideo’s website or help centre on 10 October 2026. Its MCP connection lets an AI assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT create and edit videos in its editor, and its Enterprise plan is priced on request. Videngine runs batches from a sitemap, a CSV or a JSON file, one page or row per video.

Does InVideo use stock footage or AI-generated footage?

Both. InVideo’s AI video generator draws on more than 16 million stock photos and videos, and its editor can generate images and clips with models such as Veo 3.1, Sora 2, Kling and Seedance, paid for in credits. Videngine uses neither: the pictures come from the source page or listing, or from assets you supply.

How much does InVideo cost compared with Videngine?

InVideo’s Starter, Plus and Max plans cost $10, $30 and $75 a seat a month billed annually, with 400, 2,000 and 5,000 credits per seat; its pricing page shows $20, $60 and $150 as the monthly comparison. What each video costs depends on the models and duration used. Videngine’s Growth plan is $249 a month for 250 finished videos up to 2 minutes, with seats and brands included.

Is InVideo a good online video editor or intro maker?

For single videos, yes. Its browser editor has a multitrack timeline where hand edits are free and AI edits use credits, and its intro maker generates an intro video from a prompt. Videngine isn’t a timeline editor, so for cutting your own footage or making one intro, InVideo is the better fit.
