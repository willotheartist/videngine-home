Videngine vs Canva: the short answer

Videngine is a Canva video alternative for one job: making many narrated, captioned videos from web pages, listings, products, articles or rows of data, in batches. Canva is a design and video editor where you build each video by hand from a template, with stock footage, music, captions and AI tools. Choose Canva for one-off designed videos and social graphics. Choose Videngine when every page or row needs its own video.

Deciding quickly. If someone on your team will open and shape each video, use Canva. If there are hundreds and each follows the same pattern, that’s the job Videngine is built for.

What each one is

Canva is an editor you work in; Videngine is an engine that works from your sources.

Canva

Canva is a browser-based design platform for social graphics, presentations, print and video. Its online video editor, which Canva also presents as a video maker, has a multi-layer, drag-and-drop timeline for trimming, splitting and layering clips, animations and transitions, one-click captions and a video background remover. Pro adds premium stock footage, automatic Beat Sync and Magic Resize for other aspect ratios.

Its AI tools include Magic Video, which builds an edit from clips you drop in and a short prompt, Highlights, Image to Video and an AI video generator for new clips. Text to speech turns up to 2,000 characters of script into a voiceover. Videos download as MP4 or GIF, or go straight to Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn and other networks. There is a free plan.

Videngine

Videngine is a programmatic video engine, built and run by Wall & Fifth. It turns a web page, a listing, a product, an article or a row of CSV or JSON data into a finished, narrated, captioned MP4. It writes the script, records the narration, transcribes captions from that audio and renders scenes from templates written as code, with your typefaces, colours and logo set once.

The pictures come from the source: the page’s own photos, product images or assets you supply. There is no generated footage, no stock library and no timeline, and each video is reviewed before it goes out. Videngine is launching soon; plans start at $79 a month.

Can Canva’s Bulk Create make videos?

Partly: Bulk Create fills a template’s text and image placeholders from rows of data, and videos in the generated designs stay playable, but Canva’s help pages don’t describe giving each row its own script or voiceover.

Canva’s help centre says:

  • It’s available on Pro, Teams, Business, Enterprise, Education and Nonprofits plans, on desktop only.
  • Data comes from a CSV or XLSX file, Canva Sheets, data apps such as HubSpot, or a table typed into Canva.
  • You connect up to 150 data fields to elements in the design: a separate text box for each text field, and frames or grids for images.
  • Images have to be uploaded to Canva or embedded in the spreadsheet cell. Image links in the data are treated as plain text, so a product feed’s photo URLs won’t bring the photos in.
  • The result is either one multi-page design or one design per row, grouped in a new folder in Projects. Videos show as a frozen first frame in the preview; the generated designs include them as playable video.

Two more details matter. Canva can download a multi-page design’s pages as separate MP4 files, but not for video design types, which use one continuous timeline; there, its help centre says to make a separate design per video. And Canva’s autofill API fills brand templates with text and images; its documentation says video assets aren’t supported.

None of these pages covers what makes a narrated video: a script for each row, a voiceover from it, and captions and timings that follow that voice. As Canva describes them, those are editor steps, one video at a time. Bulk Create suits social clips with swapped text and photos; a narrated video per product is more work.

What does 250 videos a month look like?

In Canva, 250 narrated videos means finishing each one in the editor; in Videngine, it means one batch run, then reviewing 250 finished videos.

Take a shop that wants a narrated video for each of 250 products, in a vertical cut for short-form video and a landscape cut for the product page, much like video ads made from product pages.

In Videngine, the same month runs like this:

  1. Set the brand template up once: typefaces, colours, logo, safe areas and motion.
  2. Choose the 250 product pages from the sitemap, or upload a CSV or JSON file with one row per product.
  3. Start the batch. Each page or row becomes one video, queued and rendered unattended; a one-minute 1080p video typically renders in a few minutes.
  4. Review each video in the studio. Change a script line and render again if needed; the rebuild is saved as a new version beside the original.
  5. Deliver the approved videos: download them, send them to a Google Drive folder you own, queue them to YouTube or, on a static site, publish each one into the page it was made from.

Which should you choose?

Canva is the better choice for one-off designed videos, social graphics and teams who want to edit by hand; Videngine is the better choice when the same kind of video has to exist for every page, product or row.

Choose Canva if

  • You make a few videos at a time and want to shape each one
  • You also need social graphics, presentations and print
  • You want stock footage, music or AI-generated clips
  • Your team likes a timeline: trim, layer, animate
  • You need a free plan, 4K export or narration in other languages

Choose Videngine if

  • Every page, product, listing or row needs its own narrated video
  • The pictures must come from the source, not stock
  • Names must be pronounced correctly and banned phrases kept out
  • You want vertical and landscape cuts without rebuilding anything
  • Videos should go to YouTube or into the page they were made from

Questions

Is Videngine a Canva video alternative?

For making many narrated videos from web pages, listings, products or data, yes. For designing a single video by hand, with stock footage, music and animation, no: Canva’s editor is built for that and Videngine has no timeline. Many teams will use both.

Can Canva bulk create videos?

Canva’s Bulk Create fills a template’s text and image fields from a CSV, XLSX file or Canva Sheet, producing one design per row or one multi-page design. Video clips already in the template stay playable. Canva’s help pages don’t describe connecting a separate script or voiceover to each row, so narration is still added in the editor.

Is the Canva video editor free?

Yes, with limits. Canva says free accounts can export videos without a watermark as long as they use only free elements, and free MP4 downloads run up to 30 minutes at up to 1080p. On Pro, paid elements export without a watermark and MP4s can run up to 2 hours at up to 4K.

Which is cheaper for 250 videos a month?

On subscription price, Canva: Pro is US$144 a year for one person. Videngine’s Growth plan is $249 a month for 250 finished videos, with script, voice and captions included. The difference is the hours: in Canva someone builds each video, in Videngine someone reviews each one.

Can Videngine edit videos like Canva?

No. Videngine has no timeline editor. You change a video by editing its script, its source data or the template, then rendering again, and the rebuild is saved as a new version beside the original.
