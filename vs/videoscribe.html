Videngine or VideoScribe?

If you need a VideoScribe alternative because every help article, product page or row of data needs its own narrated explainer, Videngine is built for that job: it reads the source, writes the script, voices and captions it, and renders motion-graphics scenes from your coded template, in batches. If you want a whiteboard or doodle video, drawn on screen and timed by a person, VideoScribe is the better tool. Videngine doesn’t draw at all.

Deciding quickly. If the video should look hand-drawn, or a teacher or trainer will build it, use VideoScribe. If the words already sit on hundreds of pages or rows and each needs its own video, use Videngine.

What each one is

VideoScribe is a whiteboard animation tool you work in by hand; Videngine is an engine that works from writing you already have.

VideoScribe

VideoScribe is whiteboard animation software for explainer videos, aimed at learning and development, HR and marketing teams, and at teachers and students. You start from a template or a blank canvas, place hand-drawn images and text, and each image draws itself as the video plays. Scenes, a timeline and camera control set the pace.

Its AI tools write a script from a prompt, generate images and turn text into a voiceover in 45+ languages, or you record your own voice. Plans are billed per licence, after a seven-day free trial.

Videngine

Videngine is a programmatic video engine built and run by Wall & Fifth. Give it a help article, a web page, a block of text or a row of CSV or JSON data and it writes a script, records the narration, transcribes captions from the finished audio and renders scenes from your brand’s coded templates.

There is no drawing hand, no timeline and no stock library, and every video is reviewed before it goes out. Videngine is launching soon; plans start at $79 a month for 50 videos.

Whiteboard animation or motion graphics?

The two make different-looking explainers: VideoScribe draws them, Videngine sets them in type, cards and pictures.

A VideoScribe video is the classic whiteboard or doodle style: hand-drawn images appear on a white background, drawn by a hand holding a pen, marker, brush or crayon you pick. Its library holds over 5 million images and GIFs.

A Videngine explainer is motion graphics: chapter title cards, information cards and side-by-side comparisons in your typefaces and colours, with narration over screenshots, product images or your own footage. It doesn’t do whiteboard, hand-drawn or character animation.

If the drawn look is the point, use VideoScribe; our guide to whiteboard video makers and when hand-drawn explainers work covers the alternatives. For the wider choice of style, see explainer videos: animated, AI and whiteboard.

VideoScribe for education and training

For classrooms, VideoScribe is the stronger choice: it is built for teachers and students to make videos themselves, and Videngine isn’t.

VideoScribe’s education page pitches it for lesson resources, assignments and student collaboration, with templates by subject, from science and maths to history. People studying or working at educational institutions can ask for a discount, from 25% and rising with the number of licences.

Videngine fits a different part of education and training: writing that already exists and needs a video for every piece of it. Course pages, procedures or a help centre become narrated, captioned explainers in one batch. That is the job of our explainer video maker; how-to videos from help articles shows it on a help centre. Videngine narrates in English only and doesn’t make SCORM packages or quizzes, or track learners.

With VideoScribe, 100 explainers means 100 projects, each built, timed and voiced by a person; with Videngine, one batch run and a review queue.

None of these steps is hard in VideoScribe; there are just a lot of them. Core allows 30 downloads a month per licence, so 100 needs Max. At half an hour a video, that is 50 hours of building.

In Videngine, the same month runs like this:

  1. Choose the sources. Pick the 100 articles from your sitemap, or upload a CSV or JSON file.
  2. Let it write. Each article gets a script and a timed scene plan; accept it, edit a line or supply your own.
  3. Voice and caption. Narration follows your pronunciation lexicon; captions come from the finished audio.
  4. Render. Batch jobs run unattended; a one-minute 1080p video typically renders in a few minutes.
  5. Review and deliver. Approve each video, then download it, send it to Google Drive or YouTube, or publish it into its article on a static site.

Which should you choose?

Choose VideoScribe when the hand-drawn look matters or a person will build each video; choose Videngine when the same kind of narrated explainer has to exist for every page, article or row.

Choose VideoScribe if

  • You want a whiteboard or doodle video
  • Teachers or students will make the videos
  • You want to time every drawing and camera move by hand
  • You need narration in languages other than English
  • You make a few videos at a time

Choose Videngine if

  • Every help article, product or page needs its own narrated explainer
  • The words already exist in pages or data
  • Product names and terms must be pronounced correctly
  • You need captions and several formats from one source
  • Videos should go to YouTube or into the page they were made from

Videngine is the wrong tool for a whiteboard animation, a character-led story or one explainer you want to shape scene by scene. It has no free plan and narrates in English only. For the drawn look, use VideoScribe; for character-led animation, see Videngine vs Animaker.

Questions

Is Videngine a VideoScribe alternative?

For making many narrated explainers from help articles, web pages or rows of data, yes: Videngine writes, voices, captions and renders each one from its source, in batches. For a whiteboard or doodle video drawn on screen, no: Videngine doesn’t draw, and VideoScribe is built for exactly that.

Is VideoScribe free?

Not beyond a trial. VideoScribe’s pricing page offers a seven-day free trial with no credit card, three video downloads and watermarked videos. Paid plans are billed per licence; on annual billing they are $150 a year for Lite, $255 for Core and $320 for Max.

Does VideoScribe have education pricing?

Yes. VideoScribe’s education page offers a discount to people studying or working at educational institutions, starting at 25% and rising with the number of licences, on monthly or annual plans. You request it from VideoScribe rather than choosing it on the pricing page.

Can VideoScribe make a video automatically from text?

Partly. Its AI tools write a script from a prompt, generate images and voice up to 4,000 characters at a time, but you place images and adjust timings on the timeline yourself. We didn’t find a way to turn a list of pages or a spreadsheet into videos; Videngine makes one video per page or row.

Which is better for whiteboard or doodle videos?

VideoScribe. Its images draw themselves on playback, with a drawing hand and pen you choose, from a library of over 5 million images and GIFs. Videngine makes motion-graphics explainers from text and doesn’t do whiteboard or hand-drawn styles.
