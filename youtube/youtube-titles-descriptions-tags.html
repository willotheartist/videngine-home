YouTube titles, descriptions and tags that get videos found

How to write YouTube titles and descriptions for business videos: what each field does per YouTube Help, hashtag and tag rules, a template and a checklist.

The short answer

Good YouTube titles and descriptions tell a searcher what a video shows before they click. YouTube's Help pages say the title, thumbnail and description matter more for discovery than tags, which play a minimal role unless your subject is often misspelled. For a business, write the title in the words people search, open the description with what the video is and who it is for, and add a few specific hashtags. Videngine writes all of this for each cut.

What each field does, according to YouTube

YouTube's Help pages rank the fields plainly: title, thumbnail and description first for discovery, tags last.

Title. YouTube's Edit video settings page sets a limit of 100 characters. Its tips for video descriptions suggest choosing one or two main words that describe the video and using them in both the title and the description.

Description. A description can run to 5,000 characters, but the first few lines carry it: they are what viewers see before “Show more”. YouTube also asks for a unique description on every video, which helps it turn up in search and stand apart from similar videos.

Tags. YouTube's page on tags says they help if your video's content is commonly misspelled and otherwise play a minimal role in discovery. It also warns that adding excessive tags to a video description is against its policies on spam, deceptive practices and scams.

Hashtags. According to YouTube's hashtag page, up to three of the hashtags in a description, the ones considered most engaging, appear by the video title. A video with more than 60 hashtags has every one ignored, hashtags contain no spaces, and hashtags unrelated to the video can lead to its removal.

Chapters. YouTube recommends chapters to help viewers navigate longer videos. A two-minute video made from one page rarely needs them; for a longer cut, check YouTube's chapter requirements first.

That is most of YouTube SEO for business: accurate words in the right fields.

Give the Short and the long-form cut different angles

A Short and a long-form cut of the same page are watched in different moments, so each needs its own title: the Short leads with one fact worth stopping for, the long-form cut with what people search.

A YouTube description template for business videos

Put the answer first, the facts second and the links last, so the lines viewers see before “Show more” already say what the video is.

[What this video shows], for [who it is for], in one sentence.
[One more line using the one or two main words people search.]

What you'll see
• [Fact from the page: size, model, year, location or version]
• [Fact from the page]
• [Fact from the page]

Full details: [link to the page this video was made from]
More like this: [link to a playlist]

[Channel line from your default description]

#SpecificHashtag #SpecificHashtag #SpecificHashtag

Take the facts from the source page, and leave out anything that changes often, such as a price, unless someone owns updating it. YouTube's tips also suggest a default description for channel information on every upload, and bullet lists for readability. Hashtags can go last: YouTube picks up to three from the description to show by the title.

A checklist before you publish

Run the same ten checks on every video, whether a person or a tool wrote the metadata.

  1. The title says what the video shows, in the source page's words, within 100 characters.
  2. The one or two main words appear in the title and in the first line of the description.
  3. The first line says what the video is and who it is for, and still makes sense when cut off.
  4. Every fact in the title and description is on the source page today: model, size, location, availability.
  5. The description is unique to this video, not a template with the title swapped in.
  6. The description links to the page the video was made from.
  7. There are three to five hashtags, each specific to this video and written without spaces.
  8. Tags cover misspellings and other names for the subject, and none are pasted into the description.
  9. The Short and the long-form cut have different titles.
  10. Privacy and any synthetic-content disclosure are set before the video goes public.

When to write metadata by hand

Write metadata by hand when the title is part of the creative work: launches, flagship videos, topical videos and anything presenter-led or not in English.

  • Launches and flagship videos. When the wording is a brand decision, a person should make it.
  • Presenter-led channels. Videngine has no avatars or on-camera presenters, and those titles trade on a personality.
  • Topical videos. A response to news needs a title written that day.
  • Other languages. Videngine narrates in English only.
  • A handful of videos a year. YouTube Studio is enough, with the Research tab in YouTube Analytics that YouTube's tips suggest for finding keywords.

YouTube titles and descriptions: questions

How long should YouTube titles and descriptions be?

YouTube allows titles of up to 100 characters and descriptions of up to 5,000. Most business videos need far less: lead the title with the main words and say what the video is in the description's first line. Videngine keeps Shorts titles to 60 characters.

Do YouTube tags still matter?

A little. YouTube's Help page says tags play a minimal role in discovery and help most when your video's content is commonly misspelled. The title, thumbnail and description matter more.

How many hashtags should a YouTube video have?

A few specific ones. YouTube shows up to three hashtags by the video title and ignores every hashtag on a video that has more than 60. Videngine writes three to five per cut.

Is there a good YouTube description template for business videos?

Yes: one sentence on what the video shows and who it is for, a few facts from the source page, a link to that page, your channel line, then a few hashtags. Keep each description unique, as YouTube's tips advise.

Should a business use a YouTube title generator?

For ideas, yes. Check each suggestion against the facts on the source page and keep the plain one a buyer would type. A title that promises what the video does not show costs you the viewer's trust.
