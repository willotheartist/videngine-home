Videngine reads the page or data row, writes the script, title and hashtags, narrates it, burns in captions and renders a 9:16 MP4 from your own photos, then publishes it to your channel once approved.

What YouTube counts as a Short

YouTube counts a video as a Short when it is square or vertical and no longer than three minutes; the file's shape and length decide, not the tool that made it.

That is the rule on YouTube Help's page on three-minute Shorts, for uploads after 15 October 2024; a wider ratio such as 16:9 keeps a video in long-form. YouTube's guide to creating Shorts adds that vertical videos saved on a computer can be uploaded as Shorts, at up to 1080p.

Short structure that works for a business: hook, facts, close

A business Short works when it opens on the subject and the fact a viewer filters on, gives three or four supporting facts, and closes on where to find the rest.

Shorts are met in a feed, so spend the first two seconds on the subject, not a logo. In the middle, give one fact per scene over a photo that shows it. Close on where the full page is, held still long enough to read.

The brand's voice rules set the target length, banned phrases and a pronunciation lexicon for model, place and brand names. Keep every claim to the source: if the listing does not say one owner, neither should the Short.

Titles and hashtags for Shorts

A Short's title should put the most useful fact first in under 60 characters, and its hashtags should name the subject, not the format.

Videngine writes YouTube metadata at the script step, separately for the Short and the long-form cut. Long-form is found by search, so its title names the thing plainly; a Short is met in a feed, so its title leads with the fact that makes someone stop.

Vertical framing and burned-in captions

A short video maker for a business has to fit landscape photos into a tall frame without cutting the subject off, and put the words on screen for people watching with the sound off.

Cropping a 3:2 listing or product photo to 9:16 keeps under 40% of its width, which loses both ends of a van or a sofa. Videngine frames each photo scene for the output format instead: a photo that does not fit sits over a blurred fill of itself, so the whole subject stays visible, with a slow pan and zoom. Vertical slideshows from landscape photos compares cropping, letterboxing and the blurred fill.

Captions are transcribed back from the finished narration, so they match what was said; for Shorts they are burned into the picture, with an .srt sidecar available. Safe areas are set once as brand tokens, with the typefaces, colours and logo, so captions and prices stay clear of the app's buttons. Check names in the captions during review.

How to make YouTube Shorts in batches

Making Shorts for a whole catalogue or archive is a batch job: fix the format once, pilot a handful, then turn every page or row into one Short and review each.

  1. Choose the sources. Pick pages, products, listings or articles from your sitemap, or prepare a CSV or JSON file with an ID, the facts and image URLs per row.
  2. Set the format once. 9:16 at 1080p, burned-in captions, brand tokens, a target length and the pronunciation lexicon.
  3. Pilot five to ten. Watch them on a phone. Fix problems in the template, voice rules or data, not in single videos.
  4. Run the batch. Jobs queue and run unattended; a one-minute 1080p video typically renders in a few minutes.
  5. Review every Short. Check facts, captions, title and hashtags against the source.
  6. Publish or deliver. Send approved Shorts to your channel, download the MP4s, or deliver them to a Google Drive folder you own.
  7. Rebuild when the source changes. A new price or a sold listing makes a Short wrong; the video ledger saves the rebuild as a new version, such as _v2, beside the original.

Publishing Shorts to your channel

Approved Shorts go from the Videngine studio to a connected YouTube channel, and nothing is uploaded until someone has reviewed it.

A channel Owner connects the channel once, then sets the defaults: privacy (public, unlisted or private) and synthetic-content disclosure, which YouTube AI disclosure covers. Unlisted lets you check the first Shorts on YouTube before they appear on the channel. Queue one Short at a time from review, all approved Shorts at once, or publish automatically on approval. Uploads are resumable and paced within YouTube's daily upload allowance, so a large batch may take more than a day.

Questions about making YouTube Shorts

What is the best YouTube Shorts maker for a business?

One that reads what you already publish, such as pages, listings, products or articles, works in batches and has a review step before anything goes out. For Shorts you film yourself on a phone, the YouTube app or a mobile editor is quicker.

Can I make YouTube Shorts from my website?

Yes. Videngine reads a page in a headless browser, keeps its text, images and structured data, and turns it into a narrated, captioned 9:16 Short. It can also read your sitemap so you can pick pages to turn into Shorts in batches.

Is Videngine an AI Shorts maker?

In part. AI writes the script, the YouTube title, description and hashtags, and the synthetic voice. The pictures come from your page, listing or supplied images, placed by coded templates, so Videngine does not invent footage.

How long can a YouTube Short be?

Up to three minutes. YouTube Help says square or vertical videos of up to three minutes uploaded after 15 October 2024 count as Shorts, while a wider ratio such as 16:9 makes a long-form video. For one listing, product or article, 20 to 45 seconds is usually enough.
