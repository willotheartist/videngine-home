YouTube AI disclosure: when to label synthetic content

YouTube AI disclosure for business videos: what YouTube's help pages say needs the AI use label, what doesn't, where the setting is and how to decide.

By Videngine10 October 20268 min read

The short answer

YouTube AI disclosure means selecting Yes under “AI use” in YouTube Studio when AI has meaningfully altered or generated realistic content: a real person appearing to say or do something they didn't, altered footage of a real place or event, a realistic scene that never happened, or AI music that is the focus of the video. An AI-written script or AI captions need no label. In Videngine, disclosure is a setting on each YouTube channel connection.

What YouTube AI disclosure covers

YouTube's policy on altered or synthetic content asks for a label when AI makes something look real that could mislead a viewer, not whenever AI touched the video.

YouTube's help page on disclosing AI content says: “we require creators to disclose when they use AI to meaningfully alter or generate photorealistic content.” It names four cases, whether the content is “fully or partially altered or created using AI tools”:

  • “Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn’t do.”
  • “Alters footage of a real event or place.”
  • “Generates a realistic scene that didn’t actually occur.”
  • “Creates music that’s the main focus of the video.”

Content that isn't realistic needs no label, and nor do minor edits, which the page defines as “primarily aesthetic” changes that “don’t alter the content in a way that could mislead the viewer about what actually happened.” Its minor list includes AI help with a script or title, “Caption creation”, colour correction, upscaling, audio repair and “Cloning one’s own voice to create voice overs or dubs”.

Read the examples as a principle, not a complete rulebook. For the wider channel plan, start at our YouTube video guide for businesses.

Where the AI use setting is, and how the label appears

Disclosure is one question in the upload flow: in the Attributes section, under “AI use”, choose Yes or No.

On a computer, YouTube's page says to go to YouTube Studio, follow the upload steps and, under “AI use”, “select Yes if your content meets the disclosure requirements or No if not.”

How the label is appliedWhere viewers may see it
You select Yes, and the AI content is photorealisticThe expanded description; “a label in the video player may also appear”
You select Yes, and the AI content is non-photorealistic or animatedThe expanded description
Made with YouTube's GenAI tools, carrying C2PA metadata, or detected by YouTube's systemsAutomatically, on the player or in the expanded description; you are notified in Studio

The companion page, Understanding ‘How this content was made’ disclosures on YouTube, names the label “Made with AI” and says it “indicates that a video contains content that has been meaningfully altered or synthetically generated.” A “How this content was made” section in the expanded description gives the details.

A wrong automatic label can “in most cases” be changed by selecting No in the AI disclosure survey under Attributes, but not on content made with YouTube's AI tools, content with C2PA metadata or content labelled after manual review. Clips from generators that write C2PA Content Credentials may arrive already labelled.

What happens if you don't disclose

YouTube says creators who consistently don't disclose may have a label applied for them, or face penalties.

In the page's words, they “may be subject to manual application of a label” or “penalties from YouTube, including removal of content or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program.” A business channel shouldn't test where “consistently” begins.

The same page says: “Disclosing AI content won’t limit a video’s audience or impact its eligibility to earn money.” By YouTube's own account, the cost of disclosing when unsure is a label, not reach. YouTube applies its Community Guidelines to all content, “including content that is AI generated or altered using AI”, so a label does not excuse a misleading video.

How to decide, video by video

Work from what is real in the video to what AI made, and stop at the first case that applies.

  1. List what is in the video: footage, photos, people, places, voice and music.
  2. Look for a real person. If anyone appears to say or do something they didn't, including through a cloned voice, select Yes.
  3. Look for real places and events. Altered footage of one, or generated extra footage of one, means Yes.
  4. Look for realistic scenes that never happened, such as a generated product demonstration. Select Yes.
  5. Check the music. AI music that is the focus of the video means Yes.
  6. Match the rest to the exempt list. If scripts, titles, captions, colour correction, upscaling, audio repair or your own cloned voice cover everything AI did, select No.
  7. If something is left over and you are unsure, disclose. A synthetic narrator is the usual leftover. Select Yes and say what was synthetic in the description: “Narrated with a synthetic voice. Photographs show the actual property.”

Decide once per kind of video, not per upload, and record the answer beside the template. If uploads are automated, decide before the queue runs; our guide to YouTube automation for businesses covers which steps to keep human.

Questions about YouTube AI disclosure

Do I need to disclose AI on YouTube if AI only wrote the script?

No. YouTube's help page lists production assistance, including using generative AI to create or improve a script, outline, title or thumbnail, as not needing disclosure. The voice and pictures are judged separately.

Does YouTube allow AI voice narration?

Neither YouTube's disclosure page nor its channel monetisation policies prohibit a synthetic narrator. The disclosure page doesn't address a stock synthetic voice reading your own script, so if unsure, select Yes under AI use. A real person's cloned voice reading lines they never recorded should be disclosed.

What does the YouTube AI label look like to viewers?

YouTube's help pages describe a Made with AI label in the expanded description, which may also appear in the video player for photorealistic content. It is added when you select Yes, and YouTube may add it automatically.

What happens if I don't use the YouTube AI label?

YouTube says creators who consistently don't disclose may have a label applied manually or face penalties, including removal of content or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program. It also says disclosing won't limit a video's audience or its eligibility to earn money.

Do Videngine videos need YouTube AI disclosure?

Videngine uses real photos from your source, not generated footage, so the pictures don't trigger YouTube's rules. The voice decides it: YouTube's page doesn't address the default synthetic narrator directly, and the Scale plan's cloned voice reproduces a real person. If unsure, set the channel connection's disclosure setting to Yes.
