AI video generator costs: compare the full production bill

Compare AI video tool costs across subscriptions, credits, generated footage, rendering, retries and review using cost per approved video.

The short answer

To compare AI video generator costs, count the full cost of producing an approved output: subscription or usage fees, extra generation attempts, narration, storage, editing and review. A monthly price alone does not show what a recurring workflow will cost.

Start by identifying the billing unit

A video tool may charge by seat, plan, credits, generated seconds, rendered duration or a combination. The units describe different work. Generating a new shot and composing existing assets into a finished video are not the same operation.

Write down the units for each shortlisted tool and check the current pricing terms directly. Also check what happens when a job fails, when unused credits expire and whether the output settings you require use a different rate.

Use cost per approved video

Calculate: total production cost divided by the number of videos that meet your acceptance criteria. Count approval consistently: the exported file is readable, accurate, correctly formatted and ready for its intended destination.

Illustrative monthly model

Tool charges: £120
Additional usage: £40
Review: 4 hours × £30 = £120
Total: £280
Approved videos: 40
Cost per approved video: £7

These are hypothetical inputs, not provider prices, a Videngine quote or a measured customer result. Replace every value with your own estimate. If only 20 outputs are approved from the same spend, the cost rises to £14 per approved video.

For different video lengths, also calculate cost per approved output minute. Use both measures: a short ad may require more creative review than a longer templated update. Duration alone does not describe the difficulty of the work.

Count versions and revisions before buying a plan

A request for 50 product videos may actually mean two aspect ratios, two languages and two hooks for each product. That produces 400 deliverables before revisions. If those dimensions do not need separate files, document which combinations are actually required rather than multiplying automatically.

Keep a modest pilot separate from the forecast. Measure how often an output needs correction and what each correction triggers. Replacing one image may require only a new render in one workflow, but a new script, voice track and edit in another.

Use the batch-volume calculation to count expected outputs. For ad hooks, follow the controlled variant approach so extra files represent useful tests rather than arbitrary differences.

Compare more than the cheapest export

A tool with a higher headline price may reduce review time if it keeps approved facts and scenes stable during revisions. A flexible editor may be more economical for a few custom videos than paying to design and maintain a reusable template.

For an automated system, include setup over a period that matches your actual plans. If a hypothetical template costs £600 to build and will produce 120 approved videos, setup contributes £5 per video. If the project stops after 20, that contribution is £30. Neither forecast should be presented as a guaranteed saving.

Remotion's documentation describes rendering capabilities rather than an all-inclusive managed production service. Shotstack documents a cloud editing API. When comparing a library, API and managed tool, account for the infrastructure and engineering you retain with each approach.

Use a trial to collect the missing numbers

  1. Prepare the same small set of representative briefs for each candidate.
  2. Record time spent preparing inputs and making the first draft.
  3. Count attempts and note why each rejected output failed.
  4. Make a realistic correction and record its time and usage.
  5. Review exports on the intended device.
  6. Estimate ongoing volume using the observed workflow, not the best single attempt.

Provider credit definitions, limits and prices can change. Keep the date of your comparison and retain the plan assumptions alongside the result. Recheck them before moving from a pilot to recurring production.

AI video pricing questions

Is a free AI video generator enough?

It may be enough to assess basic controls or make a small permitted output. Check restrictions on exports, usage, watermarking and commercial use. A free interface test is not necessarily a realistic production trial.

Is automated video always cheaper than manual editing?

No. Template setup and maintenance may not pay back for a few unique videos. Automation becomes more attractive when approved design rules are reused across enough outputs and manual repair remains low.

How should I budget for Videngine?

Start with the published launch pricing, then confirm your recipe, volume and required integrations. Compare the total workflow cost rather than treating this guide's illustrative numbers as a product quote.

Have a video job that repeats?

Bring your source material, required format and expected volume. Start with an approved example, then define a workflow you can repeat.
