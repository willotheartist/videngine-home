How to write an explainer video script

A practical method for an explainer video script of 60 to 120 seconds: the structure, word counts, writing for the ear, a sample script and a template.

The short answer

A good explainer video script for a 60 to 120 second business video has five parts: problem, answer, how it works, proof and one next step. Write it for the ear: short sentences, subject first, numbers as they are said. Budget roughly 130 to 160 spoken words a minute, as a rule of thumb. The structure of an explainer video script

A business explainer works best in five parts, in this order: problem, answer, how it works, proof and next step.

Each part does one job. Sample lines come from the script further down.

PartWhat it doesTime in 90 secondsSample line
ProblemA situation the viewer recognises. No product yet.10–20 seconds“You sent the invoice. Three weeks later, it still isn't paid.”
AnswerWhat the product is and does, in one sentence.5–10 seconds“Quietledger sends polite payment reminders for you, on a schedule you choose.”
How it worksThe steps, in the order the viewer meets them. Three is usually enough.30–40 seconds“First, add an invoice, or import a list of them.”
ProofThe claim working: a real screen, before and after, or a sourced figure.15–20 seconds“On the left is what your client sees: the amount, the due date and a link to pay.”
Next stepOne action and where to take it.5–10 seconds“Try it on your next invoice.”

Proof is the part teams most often skip: a screen counts, an adjective does not. The explainer videos hub covers animated, AI and whiteboard styles built on the same five moves.

How many words fit in 60 to 120 seconds

Budget the script in spoken words, not pages: as a rule of thumb, narration runs at roughly 130 to 160 words a minute.

It is a planning aid, not a measurement: title cards and screenshots need silent time, so plan below it.

Finished lengthWords at 130–160 a minute (rule of thumb)Safer target
60 seconds130–160120–130
90 seconds195–240170–190
120 seconds260–320230–250

Read the draft aloud with a timer. To choose between 60 and 120 seconds, see explainer video length and cost.

Write for the ear, not the page

Write each sentence so it makes sense the first time it is heard, because the viewer can't reread it.

  • Short sentences. One idea each, most under fifteen words.
  • Subject first. “Quietledger sends reminders”, not “Designed for small firms, Quietledger…”.
  • Numbers said aloud. Write “ten forty-two” or “twelve hundred and fifty pounds”, and put the figure on screen.
  • Say what the picture shows. W3C's guidance on description of visual information advises: “When writing the script, make sure all relevant visual information is included.”
Written for the pageWritten for the ear
“Reminders are dispatched at T+0, T+7 and T+14.”“It sends a reminder on the due date, then a week later, then a week after that.”
“As shown in fig. 2, overdue items are flagged.”“Overdue invoices are marked in red, at the top of your list.”

A synthetic voice needs a pronunciation list for names and acronyms; see AI voiceover for video. To cut a long article down to a script, see article to video.

A sample script, with scene notes

Here is a complete 90-second script for an explainer video, with scene notes. Quietledger is an imaginary invoice-reminder tool, invented for this guide; nothing here describes a real company or result.

TimePartScene noteNarration
0:00–0:12ProblemTitle card, “Late invoices”, then a card: “Invoice 1042: sent 21 days ago”.“You finished the job. You sent the invoice. Three weeks later, it still isn't paid, and now it's your job to chase it.”
0:12–0:20ProblemCard: “Write. Send. Remember. Repeat.”“Chasing by hand takes time, it feels awkward, and the invoice that matters most is the one you forget.”
0:20–0:29AnswerName card and logo.“Quietledger sends polite payment reminders for you, on a schedule you choose, and stops as soon as you're paid.”
0:29–0:37How it worksTitle card, “How it works”, then the invoice list, slow zoom to Add invoice.“Here's how it works. First, add an invoice, or import a list of them.”
0:37–0:49How it worksScreenshot: the schedule settings.“Second, pick a schedule. For example: a friendly note on the due date, a firmer one a week later, and a final one a week after that.”
0:49–1:00How it worksScreenshot: an email preview, open for editing.“Third, read each email before it goes, and change any word you like. When the client pays, mark the invoice paid, and the reminders stop.”
1:00–1:17ProofSide by side: the reminder in the client's inbox, and the list showing Paid.“On the left is what your client sees: the amount, the due date and a link to pay. On the right is your list the next morning, with invoice ten forty-two marked paid.”
1:17–1:27Next stepCard: “Try it on your next invoice” and the address, held for three seconds.“Try it on your next invoice. Set up your first schedule at the address on screen.”

The narration is 176 words, under the safer target, leaving silent time for cards and screens. In a real script, the proof must be a genuine screen, not a mock-up.

A template and a review checklist

Copy this explainer video script template, fill one block per part, then run the checklist.

EXPLAINER SCRIPT: [product or feature]
For: [viewer]    Action: [one thing to do after]
Length: [60 / 90 / 120] s   Words: [safer target]

1. PROBLEM       10-20 s   25-45 words
   Screen: [title card, or the problem shown]
   Say:    [the situation, in the viewer's words]

2. ANSWER        5-10 s    10-20 words
   Screen: [name card or logo]
   Say:    [Product] [does what] for [whom].

3. HOW IT WORKS  30-40 s   60-90 words
   Screen: [one screenshot or card per step]
   Say:    First, [step]. Second, [step]. Third, [step].

4. PROOF         15-20 s   30-45 words
   Screen: [real screen, before and after, sourced figure]
   Say:    [what the viewer is looking at, and why]

5. NEXT STEP     5-10 s    10-20 words
   Screen: [action and address, held 3 s or more]
   Say:    [one action]. [where to take it].

SAY IT RIGHT: [name or number] = [how it is spoken]
SOURCE: [page this came from, and its date]

The word budgets assume about 140 words a minute, less silent time. Before recording, check:

  1. Read aloud at recording pace, with a timer. Cut, don't speed up.
  2. The five parts are in order, and the product is named within thirty seconds.
  3. Each sentence carries one idea and starts with its subject.
  4. Numbers and names are written as spoken, with exact figures on screen.
  5. Every claim in the proof can be shown or sourced.
  6. There is one next step, its address on screen for three seconds or more.
  7. Someone new to the product can say back what it does after one read-through.

When to hire a scriptwriter instead

Hire a scriptwriter when the hard part is deciding what to say, not saying it clearly.

  • The answer isn't settled. If nobody can write the one-sentence answer, a scriptwriter who talks to customers will find it faster than any tool.
  • It's a brand film or one flagship video. Story, humour and character are craft work for a scriptwriter with an animation studio or production company.
  • You want characters, whiteboard drawing or a presenter. Videngine animates type, cards and your own images, and has no avatars. See animated explainer options, or an avatar presenter tool such as Synthesia (our comparison).

Explainer script questions

What should an explainer video script include?

Five parts, in order: the problem the viewer recognises, a one-sentence answer, how it works in about three steps, proof the viewer can see, and one next step. How it works takes the most time.

How many words is a script for a 90-second explainer video?

As a rule of thumb, narration runs at roughly 130 to 160 words a minute, so 90 seconds holds about 195 to 240 words. Plan for 170 to 190, because title cards and screenshots need silent time.

Is there a simple explainer video script template?

Yes. Write the five parts as numbered blocks, each with a time, a word budget, what is on screen and the line to be said. Add how names and numbers are spoken. The template on this page copies as plain text.

How is video script writing different from writing a web page?

A listener can't scroll back, so each sentence must make sense the first time it is heard. Use short sentences with the subject first, say numbers the way people say them, and describe what the viewer needs from the screen.

It can draft one from material you already have, such as a feature page or help article. In Videngine you can accept the draft, edit a line or supply your own script word for word. A person should still check every claim before it is recorded.
