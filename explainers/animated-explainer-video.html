Animated explainer videos: motion graphics, characters or whiteboard

An animated explainer video can be motion graphics, character animation or whiteboard. Compare the three by job, effort and scale before choosing a tool.

The short answer

An animated explainer video is one of three different things: motion graphics, where type, shapes, screenshots and images move; character animation, where illustrated people act out a story; or whiteboard, where a hand draws the explanation. Choose by the job. Motion graphics suit software, products and anything that changes; characters suit stories about people; whiteboard suits a process or an idea. Which style suits software, products, processes, training and ads

Match the style to what the viewer must recognise afterwards: a screen or an object points to motion graphics, a situation between people points to characters, and a sequence of ideas points to whiteboard.

For software and training, the deciding question is what happens next month. A motion graphics explainer built from screenshots and text can be rendered again from the updated source; a drawn version has to be redrawn and retimed. Cost and effort, without price tags

The cost of an animated explainer is mostly people's time, and the style decides where that time goes: once at the start, on every video, or every time the subject changes.

Agencies usually quote per project and software charges by plan, so compare what each price includes: script, voice, revisions and changes after delivery.

A flagship brand explainer can justify bespoke character work because it is made once. A library of fifty help explainers rarely can, because keeping each one accurate is a cost that repeats. How to make a motion graphics explainer

A motion graphics explainer takes eight steps, and most of its quality is decided before anything moves.

  1. Name one viewer and one action. “A new admin sets up single sign-on” is a brief; “our security features” is a topic.
  2. Collect the real material: the source page, current screenshots, product photos and the logo.
  3. Write the script for the ear: the problem, how it works, one example, one next step. Read it aloud and time it.
  4. Plan the scenes, one idea each: a title card per chapter, an information card for a figure or menu path, a side-by-side comparison for before and after, a screenshot for the interface.
  5. Set the brand once: typefaces, colours, logo, safe areas and motion.
  6. Record the voice after deciding how product names and acronyms are said.
  7. Caption from the finished audio, so the captions match what was said.
  8. Render and review: 16:9 for a web page, 9:16 for Shorts. Watch with sound and without, and note which version of the source each video came from.

Animated explainer questions

What is an animated explainer video?

It is a short video that explains one thing with animation rather than filmed footage. The animation is usually one of three styles: motion graphics, character animation or whiteboard. Narration carries the explanation, and the animation shows what the narration describes.

Which animated explainer video style is best for software?

Usually motion graphics with real screenshots, because the viewer needs to recognise the screen when they open the product. Screenshots can also be swapped and the video rendered again when the interface changes.

How do I create an animated video without an animator?

Pick a tool built for the style you need: a character animation tool, a whiteboard tool, or a motion graphics tool that works from templates. Write the script first, collect real images, and let the template carry the design. For many explainers from existing pages, Videngine builds each one from its source and a fixed set of scenes.

Is an AI animation video generator the same as an animated explainer video maker?

Not always. An AI animation video generator usually creates the scenes or characters from a prompt, while an animated explainer video maker may only assemble your script, images and brand into a video. Videngine is the second kind: AI writes the script and the voice, and the pictures come from your own material.

Does Videngine make character or whiteboard animation?

No. Videngine makes motion graphics explainers: animated type, cards, comparisons and your own images, with narration and captions. For characters, a tool such as Animaker or Powtoon fits better; for whiteboard, a tool such as VideoScribe.
