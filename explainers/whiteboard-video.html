The short answer

A whiteboard video maker draws simple pictures line by line, as if by hand, in time with a narrator. The format works for processes, abstract concepts and training, where watching an idea being built helps it make sense. It works far less well for products, listings or anything that must show the real thing. Videngine does not make whiteboard videos: it turns documents into motion-graphics explainers, which suits teams that need many.

What a whiteboard video is, and why it holds attention

A whiteboard video is a narrated explainer in which simple illustrations appear to be drawn onto a white surface while a voice explains them, often with a hand holding the pen.

Doodle video, whiteboard animation and whiteboard explainer video all describe the same style. With a whiteboard video maker, nobody draws each frame: the software takes line art from its library, or an image you upload, and animates a pen tracing it.

It holds attention through a few plain mechanics:

  • Anticipation: each picture is unfinished until the last stroke, so there is a reason to watch the next few seconds.
  • One thing at a time: the board shows only what has been drawn so far, so the eye follows the pen while the narration describes it.
  • Little visual noise: black line on white strips out detail that doesn't matter.
  • A human trace: a drawing hand feels like a lesson, not an advert.

Part of the effect is novelty, so a long run of near-identical doodle videos can stop feeling fresh. Our explainer video guides compare it with animated and AI explainers.

When whiteboard animation works, and when it doesn't

Whiteboard animation works when the subject has no single real picture, and fails when the viewer needs to see the actual thing.

A process or a policy has no photograph, so a diagram drawn step by step is an honest way to show it. A car or a software screen does have one, and a sketch makes the viewer imagine what they could have been shown.

The second test is change. If the facts in a whiteboard video change monthly, every change is a small redraw, so keep prices, specifications and dates out of the script.

How to script and storyboard a whiteboard explainer

Write the narration first, break it into beats that each earn one drawing, and only then open a whiteboard video maker.

Our guide to writing an explainer video script covers the writing in depth; this is the whiteboard version.

  1. State the idea in one sentence. If it takes two, you probably have two videos.
  2. Write the full narration before drawing anything. Short spoken sentences, one point each. Read it aloud and time it.
  3. Split the script into beats. A beat is a clause that earns one drawing.
  4. Sketch each beat on paper: what is drawn, what is said, what stays on the board.
  5. Choose drawings that carry meaning. Arrows, before and after, a recurring figure. Keep labels to a word or two.
  6. Plan the board so the finished frame reads as a summary.
  7. Record the narration, then time each drawing to its line, not the other way round.
  8. Review twice: sound off to check the pictures carry the sequence, sound on to check every fact.

When you need many explainers from documents

If you need an explainer for every help article, policy or product line, hand-built whiteboard videos are hard to keep up, and template-built motion graphics are the practical alternative.

A whiteboard video is crafted: someone storyboards and times each one, and reopens it when the source changes. That suits one flagship explainer, not a help centre. Motion graphics built from templates decide the design once and fill the scenes from each source text. Whiteboard video questions

What is the best whiteboard video maker?

There isn't one best tool; it depends on what you need around the whiteboard. VideoScribe and Doodly present themselves as whiteboard software first, while Animaker adds whiteboard characters and Powtoon sits whiteboard beside other styles. Try two on the same short script, and check current pricing on each tool's own site.

Is a doodle video the same as a whiteboard video?

In practice, yes. Doodle video, whiteboard animation and whiteboard explainer video name the same style: line drawings that appear to be drawn while a narrator explains them. Blackboard or glassboard backgrounds change the look, not the method.

Can Videngine make whiteboard animation?

No. Videngine makes motion-graphics explainers from text, with title cards, information cards, comparisons and your own images. It does not draw, animate hands or characters, or produce a hand-drawn style.

What is a good VideoScribe alternative?

It depends on why you are moving. If you still want whiteboard animation, Animaker, Powtoon and Doodly all make it. If the real need is many explainers from documents you already have, a template-based motion-graphics tool such as Videngine fits better.

How long should a whiteboard explainer video be?

Long enough for one idea and no longer. Write the script first and time it read aloud at recording pace. If it runs well past two minutes, it probably covers more than one idea and should become two videos.
