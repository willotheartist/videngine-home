The short answer

An AI explainer video maker uses AI for the production work: drafting a spoken script from your text, planning scenes with timings, reading the narration in a synthetic voice and writing captions from the audio. It cannot tell whether the explanation is right; a person who knows the subject still has to check it. Videngine builds motion-graphics explainers this way from a page or text you already have, using your own images rather than generated footage.

What AI does in an AI explainer video

In an AI explainer video, AI does the drafting and the production, and a person stays responsible for whether the explanation is correct.

A clumsy sentence is obvious on the first watch. A missing condition or an invented number is not, and it reaches every viewer.

Four kinds of explainer video AI, and their risks

Explainer video AI falls into four approaches, which differ less in how the script is written than in where the pictures come from.

The deciding question is what the viewer needs to see. If the subject is your product or process, the pictures should be the real thing, not invented footage. Our text-to-video AI guide separates generating footage from narrating a script, and animated explainer videos compares the visual styles.

Labelling depends on the approach too. YouTube's help page on disclosing AI-generated content says non-realistic content made with AI needs no disclosure, and lists production help such as a script, caption creation and idea generation among uses that need no label. Realistic content, such as a scene that did not happen, does. The list is not exhaustive, so check it before publishing a realistic presenter or synthetic voice.

How to brief an AI explainer video maker

Give the AI three things: one question the video answers, the source it must stay within, and the audience.

  • One question per video. A video that answers two questions answers both less well, and is harder to update. If the source covers five tasks, that is five videos.
  • The source. A specific page or block of text, not a topic, so the draft comes from what you wrote. Cut a long source to the part that answers the question; turning an article into a video covers that edit.
  • The audience. Who is watching and what they already know. Without it, a model pitches at a generic reader.
An example brief

Question: What will I pay if I upgrade part-way through a month?

Source: the help article on changing plans, current version only.

Audience: account owners on a monthly plan. They know their plan name, not the word “proration”.

Next step: open the billing page, which shows the exact amount before you confirm.

Leave out: annual plans and refunds, which have their own articles.

The last line matters: without it, a helpful model may add a statement about refunds that is true of some businesses and false of yours. Accuracy review: a checklist before anything goes out

Review an AI explainer against its source, line by line, with the person who owns the subject; reading it for tone is a different check.

Google's guidance on using generative AI content, written for web pages, gives the reason: generative models don't retrieve facts but predict a likely sequence of words, so manual fact-checking before publishing is critical. A narrated video needs the same.

  1. Check the source is current. The AI is only as right as the page it read; if the article is behind the product, fix the article first.
  2. Trace every claim to the source. Cut anything you cannot point to, or have the subject owner confirm it. Plausible extra detail is the hardest error to spot.
  3. Look for dropped conditions. Shortening removes qualifiers such as “admins only” or “within 30 days”, and a true statement becomes false.
  4. Check numbers, names and versions on the cards and in the narration.
  5. Match pictures to words. Screenshots should show today's interface, and each comparison the right thing on each side.
  6. Listen with the sound on for pronunciation, and for stress that changes the meaning.
  7. Watch with the sound off. A viewer on mute gets only the captions and on-screen text.
  8. Check the title and description. They are AI-written too.
  9. Get sign-off from the subject owner, the person who would answer the support ticket.

Record which version of the source each video came from, so you know when a video is out of date.

AI explainer video questions

What is an AI explainer video?

An AI explainer video is a short explanatory video in which AI handles some of the production: the script, voice, captions, scene plan or pictures. Someone who knows the subject still has to check that the explanation is right.

Can an explainer video generator write the script from my document?

Yes, provided you give it one question to answer, the source to stay within and the audience. Check every claim against the source before rendering. In Videngine you can accept the drafted script, edit a line, or paste your own script to be used word for word.

How accurate is explainer video AI?

As accurate as its source and its review. Language models predict likely wording rather than look facts up, so a draft can drop a condition or add a plausible detail. Trace each claim to the source and have the subject owner approve the video.

Do I have to disclose that an explainer video was made with AI?

It depends on the platform and on what the AI made. YouTube's help page says AI used for a script or captions needs no disclosure, while realistic synthetic content does. When Videngine publishes to YouTube, synthetic-content disclosure is a setting.

What is the best AI video explainer approach for a software product?

Usually motion graphics over real screenshots, because the viewer needs to see the interface as it really looks. Generated footage cannot show your product, and an avatar presenter adds a face but not the screen. For a live click-by-click walkthrough, use a screen recorder.
