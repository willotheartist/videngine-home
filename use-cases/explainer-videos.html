Explainer video maker: turn your writing into clear explainers

The short answer

An explainer video maker turns a written explanation into a short narrated video that covers a problem, how something works, an example and a next step. The strongest source is usually a document you already have: a help article, release note, feature page or training guide. Videngine reads that page or text, writes a spoken script and scene plan, and renders it with your brand's cards, images and captions as an MP4 for review.

What an explainer video is for, and a structure that works

An explainer video exists to make one thing understood well enough that the viewer can act on it. Most good explainers make the same four moves.

  • Problem: the situation the viewer recognises. “Every Monday, someone exports the same report by hand.”
  • How it works: the steps or the mechanism, in the order the viewer would meet them.
  • Proof or example: one concrete case, a screenshot of the result, or a before and after.
  • Next step: one action and where to take it. A link to the full article beats “find out more”.

Skip the example and the viewer has to take the narration on trust.

Most teams already have the source: a feature page, a help article or an educational guide for customers. The job is to adapt it, not to invent it.

Explainer typeSource you already haveSensible length
Product or feature explainerThe feature page or product page60–90 seconds
Help-centre articleThe article itself45–90 seconds, one task
Release noteThe changelog entry or release post30–60 seconds
Onboarding or training moduleThe written procedure or handbook section1–2 minutes per task, as a short series
Report or guide summaryThe executive summary or the guide60–120 seconds

Lengths are guidance, not rules. Stop when the viewer can act, and if an explanation needs longer, split it into a short series. Ads, short social clips and listing videos are different jobs, covered separately in the video use cases hub.

Write the script for the ear, not the page

A sentence that reads well on a page often sounds wrong aloud, so write for a listener who cannot scroll back.

  • One idea per scene. If a scene needs “and also”, it is two scenes.
  • Short sentences. Put the subject and verb early. Replace “as described above” with the thing itself; a listener has no “above”.
  • Numbers said the way people say them. “Version four point two”, “about two thirds”, “nine in the morning”. Put the exact figure on a card.
  • A pronunciation lexicon. Decide once how product names, acronyms and place names are said, including whether SSO is spelled out.
Illustrative rewrite

Written: “Users with Administrator permissions can now configure SAML-based SSO via the Security tab (v4.2+).”

Spoken: “If you're an admin, you can now set up single sign-on. Open Settings, then Security. You'll need version four point two or later.”

In Videngine each brand keeps its own voice rules: a pronunciation lexicon, banned phrases, positive-only wording and a target length. The script step follows them, so the fiftieth explainer sounds like the first.

For pace, a rule of thumb is roughly 130–160 spoken words per minute, so a 60-second explainer holds about 130–160 words before pauses. Technical terms and silent title cards take time, so plan near the lower end and listen before approving.

The scenes an explainer is built from

Videngine's guide template builds explainers from four kinds of scene, animated with type, cards and image motion rather than generated footage.

  • Chapter title cards mark each move: the problem, how it works, the example.
  • Information cards carry one statement, figure or short list, such as the exact menu path.
  • Side-by-side comparison scenes show before and after, or the old setting beside the new one.
  • Narration over your own material: screenshots, product images, photos or footage from the source or supplied by you. An image that does not fit the frame sits over a blurred fill of itself rather than being cropped, and moves with a slow pan and zoom.

Typefaces, colours, logo, safe areas and motion are set once as tokens, and templates are written as code (Remotion and React scenes). That is programmatic video in practice: one set of scene rules for every explainer.

Using Videngine as an explainer video maker

In Videngine, an explainer starts from a page or text you already have and ends as a reviewed MP4 file.

  1. Choose the source. A help article or feature page URL, plain text or markdown, or a batch of pages picked from your sitemap.
  2. Read the source. A headless browser keeps the text, images and structured data, and drops navigation and cookie banners.

Training and internal video: keep the document in charge

For training and internal explainers, the written document should remain the source of truth, and the video should be a rendering of it.

When a procedure changes, change the document first, then re-render the video from it, so the two never drift apart.

  • Versions: Videngine saves a rebuild as a new version, such as _v2, beside the original. Record which document revision each version came from.
  • One task per video: a module of five short videos is easier to maintain than one long one. A change to step three means re-rendering step three.
  • Captions: W3C's captions guidance describes captions as a text version of the speech and non-speech audio needed to understand the content, and distinguishes open captions, always displayed, from closed captions viewers can turn off. Burned-in captions are open; an .srt sidecar gives closed captions where your player supports it.
  • Voice: a synthetic voice keeps re-renders consistent. A recorded voice means re-recording whenever the procedure changes.

Shortening a long document is the same editorial work as adapting a blog post; our guide to turning an article into a video covers it. If you are weighing AI training video tools, our text-to-video AI guide explains the difference between generating footage and assembling a narrated script.

What “animated explainer” means here

Videngine animates type, cards and images with motion; it does not make character animation or whiteboard drawings, and it has no avatar presenter.

A search for an animated explainer video maker usually means one of three things:

  • Motion graphics over your own material: titles, cards, comparisons and screenshots that move. This is Videngine.
  • Character or whiteboard animation: illustrated people, drawn scenes, a hand sketching on a board. Use a character animation tool or a motion designer.
  • A presenter on screen: an AI avatar reading the script. Use an avatar presenter tool; our comparison of Videngine and Synthesia shows where that kind of tool fits.

Nor is Videngine a generative explainer video generator: it does not invent footage, so every picture shows your real material, such as the interface as it looks today.

Explainer video questions

How do I create an explainer video from a help article?

Pick one task the article explains and script it for a listener: the problem, the steps, an example of the result and a next step. Use the article's screenshots as the pictures. In Videngine you paste the article's URL, approve or edit the script and scene plan, and review the render before it goes out.

How long should an explainer video be?

Long enough for the viewer to act, and no longer. As guidance, 30–60 seconds suits a release note and 60–90 seconds a feature explainer. If the subject needs several minutes, a short series with one task per video is easier to watch and to update.

Can an AI explainer video maker make animated characters?

Some can, but Videngine does not. It animates type, cards, comparisons and your own images; it does not draw characters, make whiteboard animation or generate footage. For characters, use a character animation tool; for a presenter on screen, use an avatar presenter tool.

Can I use an AI training video generator for internal training?

Yes, for training that explains a written procedure: narrated, captioned videos built from the document and re-rendered when it changes. It fits less well when training needs a known person on camera, a live software walkthrough or interactive quizzes.

Is an explainer video maker the same as a video presentation maker?

Not quite. A video presentation maker usually starts from slides and narrates them in order. Videngine starts from a page, text or data and writes its own scene plan, so if your source is a slide deck, a presentation tool that exports video fits better.
