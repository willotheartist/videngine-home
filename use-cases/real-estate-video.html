Real estate listing video: a narrated video for every property

A real estate listing video for every property: narrated, captioned 9:16 and 16:9 cuts from the listing's photos and facts, kept accurate as prices change.

The short answer

A real estate listing video is a short narrated walk-through of one property, built from the listing's own photos and facts: location, bedrooms, bathrooms, size, price or rent, outside space and how to enquire. Because those already exist, every listing can have one, not only the expensive ones. Videngine reads the listing URL, drafts the script and renders a captioned vertical cut for social and a landscape cut for YouTube and your website.

Why every listing deserves a video, not just the expensive ones

Every listing can carry a video because the costly work, photographing the property and writing up its facts, is already done. Filmed real estate marketing video has usually been kept for the top of the market, because a shoot costs much the same for a one-bedroom flat as for a five-bedroom house.

  • Listing volume. Agencies, lettings teams and portals add properties every week. Hand editing does not keep up; starting from the listing URL can.
  • The photos already exist. A listing video reuses the portal photos in walk-through order instead of a second shoot.
  • Two shapes from one source. For property video on social media, a vertical 9:16 cut suits Reels, TikTok and Shorts; see short-form video for social. A landscape 16:9 cut suits YouTube, your website and portals that accept video; see YouTube videos for businesses.

We will not quote a figure for how much video lifts enquiries; compare your own listings with and without it. Property is one of several Videngine use cases, and one of the simplest: one property, a set of facts, a set of photos.

From listing URL to video: the workflow

In Videngine, one listing goes in and two cuts come out: paste the URL, check the script, photo order and pronunciation, then render and deliver.

  1. Choose the listing. Paste the listing URL, or have Videngine read your site's sitemap and choose listings from it. A headless browser reads the page, keeping text, photos and structured data and dropping navigation and cookie banners.
  2. Check the script and scene plan. Videngine drafts the narration and a timed scene plan. Accept it, edit a line or supply your own script, and strike anything the listing does not say.
  3. Set the photo order. Follow a walk-through: front, entrance, living space, kitchen, bedrooms, bathrooms, outside. In the vertical cut, a landscape photo sits over a blurred fill of itself, so the room is not cropped away.
  4. Fix pronunciation. Add street, area and development names to the brand's pronunciation lexicon; Marylebone in London and Houston Street in Manhattan are not said as they are spelt. Each entry carries into every later video.
  5. Review the draft. Watch once with sound and once without. Captions are transcribed from the finished audio, so they match the voice.
  6. Render both cuts. A 9:16 and a 16:9 MP4 (H.264), up to 1080p, with captions burned in or as an .srt file. A one-minute video typically renders in a few minutes.
  7. Deliver. Download the files, send them to a Google Drive folder you own, or publish to your connected YouTube channel as public, unlisted or private. Videngine writes separate YouTube titles, descriptions and tags for the Short and the long-form cut.

Keeping listing videos accurate when prices and status change

A listing video describes one property at one moment, so treat a price or status change as a reason to re-render or retire it, not to patch it.

Prices are reduced, rents change, and properties go under offer (in the US, pending), let agreed or sold. A video quoting the old price can circulate on social long after the listing page is updated.

  • Set a price policy per template. Either state the price or rent and re-render when it changes, or leave it out and send viewers to the listing for the current figure.
  • Re-render from the source. Update the listing, then render again. The rebuild is saved as a new version, such as _v2, beside the original, and re-rendering the same video in the same month is free.
  • Retire old versions where they live. Replace the earlier file on YouTube, social accounts and your website, and take the video down after a sale or let.

Advertising must not mislead, so keep the narration to facts in the listing: no unsupported adjectives, no unmeasured distances, no photos of a different unit. The brand's banned-phrases list blocks wording your compliance lead has ruled out.

Rules differ by country. In the US, the Department of Housing and Urban Development states that discriminatory housing advertisements are illegal under the Fair Housing Act, including adverts indicating a preference based on characteristics such as familial status or disability. Describe the property, not the people you imagine living in it. In the UK, the National Trading Standards Estate and Letting Agency Team published guidance on Part A material information for property listings in 2022; Part A includes the price or rent, the council tax band and, for sales, tenure. A short video cannot carry everything a listing must, so link to the full listing, and check your obligations with your compliance lead or trade body.

Questions about real estate listing videos

How do I make a real estate video from listing photos?

Start from the listing's photos, facts and URL. Put the photos in walk-through order, write narration that only states what the listing says, and add captions for people watching without sound. Videngine does this from the listing URL and renders a vertical and a landscape cut for review.

How long should a property listing video be?

Long enough to cover the key facts and main rooms, short enough to hold attention on a phone. Around 30 to 60 seconds suits most listings; a large house or new development may need longer. In Videngine, a video up to two minutes counts as one video.

Can one listing video work on social media, YouTube and property portals?

One listing can feed all three, but one file rarely suits them all. Social feeds suit a vertical 9:16 cut with burned-in captions, while YouTube and agent websites suit a 16:9 landscape cut. Property portals set their own rules for video, so check each one before uploading.

What should happen to a listing video when the price changes or the property sells?

Re-render it from the updated listing, or take it down. After a price change, render a new version and replace the old file wherever it was published; after a sale or let, retire the video. In Videngine the new render is saved beside the original, and re-rendering the same video in the same month is free.

Is Videngine an AI real estate video maker that generates footage?

No. AI writes the script and the narration voice, but the pictures are the listing's own photos, framed for each format with slow pan and zoom. Videngine does not invent rooms, views or footage, and it has no avatars or presenters.
