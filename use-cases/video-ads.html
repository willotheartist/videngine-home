The short answer

An AI video ad maker turns material you already have, such as a listing, a product page, an offer or a row of data, into short narrated ads in the formats each placement needs. It works best for ads whose structure repeats while the facts change. Videngine does this from coded templates: AI writes the script and voice, the pictures come from your own photos, and every ad is reviewed before it goes out.

Which marketing videos automate well, and which need a shoot

Automate ads whose structure repeats and whose facts already exist in a page or feed; commission a shoot or an editor for anything that needs new footage or a new idea.

A steady supply of ads for the things you sell is a production problem. One memorable piece about who you are is a creative one. Automation helps a great deal with the first and very little with the second.

The dividing line is new footage. If the evidence already exists as photos, a page or a feed, a template can arrange, narrate and size it. If nobody has filmed it, no template will create honest evidence of it.

Anatomy of a 15–20 second ad built from existing material

A short ad from existing material needs four beats: a hook that names the thing, visible evidence, the offer or key fact, and one call to action.

The hook is a specific fact, not a superlative. The evidence is a photo that supports it. The offer is the price, date or condition that makes the ad worth acting on now. The call to action names one next step. Four beats fit into 15 to 20 seconds while giving each photo time to register.

Here is an illustrative plan for a used-vehicle dealer, built from one stock listing. It is a brief, not a tested campaign, and the figures are placeholders.

TimeBeatNarrationPicture from the listing
0–3 secondsHook“A one-owner estate with a full service history.”Front three-quarter photo with make and model on a title card
3–9 secondsEvidence“Two keys, a stamped service book and a clean interior.”Service book, keys and interior photos, each with a slow pan and zoom
9–14 secondsOffer or fact“Priced at £11,495, with 42,000 miles on the clock.”Price and mileage card drawn from the listing’s own fields
14–18 secondsCall to action“See the full listing and book a test drive.”Dealer logo, stock reference and web address

A property agency uses the same beats with different fields: the street-facing photo as the hook, kitchen and garden as evidence, asking price and bedrooms as the fact, a viewing request as the call to action.

Listing photos are usually landscape, so in a vertical cut Videngine frames each over a blurred fill of itself rather than cropping the car out of shot. Captions are transcribed from the finished narration, so they match what the voice says.

Which aspect ratio for which placement

Use 9:16 for full-screen vertical placements, 4:5 or 1:1 for feeds, and 16:9 for YouTube and websites.

Aspect ratioWhere it usually runsWhat to watch
9:16 verticalFull-screen vertical placements such as Reels, Stories, Shorts and TikTokThe app draws buttons and text over the edges; keep titles and logos central.
4:5 verticalMobile feedsTakes more screen than square; keep overlays off the photo’s subject.
1:1 squareFeeds where one file has to serve mobile and desktopLess height for text; keep on-screen lines short.
16:9 landscapeYouTube in-stream and long-form, website embeds, landing pagesThe most room for photos and text, but small inside a vertical feed.

Google’s video ad specs for Google Ads recommend 16:9 for horizontal, 9:16 for vertical and 1:1 for square, and also accept 4:5 vertical. Lengths, file sizes and safe zones differ by platform and change, so check each platform’s own help pages before a campaign.

How to run an AI video ad maker with approvals built in

Treat the source as the record of truth and put a human review between every render and the ad account.

  1. Pick the source. A listing URL, an offer page, a block of text or a CSV row with price and image links. The URL-to-video workflow covers what to check on a single page.
  2. Extract the content. Videngine reads the page in a headless browser, keeps text, images and structured data, and drops navigation and cookie banners.
  3. Check the script against the source. The studio writes a script and timed scene plan; accept it, edit a line or supply your own. Compare every price, date and claim with the page or feed.
  4. Render the formats you need. Typefaces, colours, logo and motion come from brand tokens. Narration is a synthetic voice by default, or your own audio.
  5. Review each file before it goes out. Watch it with sound and without, and check the offer matches the landing page.
  6. Export and upload. Download the MP4s or deliver them to a Google Drive folder you own, then upload them to the ad platform yourself.
  7. Re-render when the offer changes. A rebuild is saved as a new version, such as _v2, beside the original. Re-rendering the same video in the same month is free on every plan.

Brand voice rules help: a pronunciation lexicon gets model and place names right, and a banned-phrases list keeps unsupported claims such as “best price in town” out of the script. They reduce errors; they do not replace the review.

Ad variants that answer a question

Change one thing per variant so that, when results come in, you know which change made the difference.

An AI video ad generator makes twenty versions of an ad cheap. Twenty versions that change hook, photo order, music and call to action at once produce a winner nobody can explain. Decide the question first, then render only the variants that answer it.

  • Hook test: two opening lines, such as service history against low mileage, with everything else fixed.
  • Evidence test: with the hook fixed, interior photos against the service book.
  • Call-to-action test: “Book a test drive” against “See the full listing”, with everything else fixed.
  • Format adaptation: 9:16 and 4:5 cuts of one ad are a delivery requirement, not a test.

Name every output so a result traces back to the creative. A pattern such as stock4521_hook-history_cta-testdrive_9x16_v1 records the source, the tested elements, the format and the version, and the video ledger keeps every version you tested.

Videngine makes the files; buying media and measuring results happen in the ad platform, which is why the file name matters.

When Videngine is the wrong tool

Videngine is the wrong choice when the ad depends on a person on camera, on footage that does not exist, or on one carefully crafted film.

  • Avatar or presenter ads: a digital presenter speaking to camera needs an avatar tool. See Videngine vs Synthesia.
  • UGC-style creator ads: their value is a real person using the product in their own words. Brief creators directly.
  • One hero brand film: a new idea with original footage belongs with an editor or a production company. Videngine has no timeline editor and no stock library.
  • Generated cinematic footage: scenes that were never shot are a generative model’s job. See Videngine vs AI video generators.
  • Your own template system: if your developers want to design templates and call a rendering API themselves, see Videngine vs Creatomate.

Nor does Videngine run campaigns: it has no ad-platform connection, no analytics and no scheduling to networks other than YouTube.

Questions about AI video ad makers

Can an AI video ad maker build ads from my existing listings or product pages?

Yes, if the page holds the facts and photos the ad needs. Videngine reads the page in a headless browser, keeps the text, images and structured data, and writes a script and scene plan you can accept or edit. It cannot add evidence that is not on the page, such as a demonstration nobody filmed.

What aspect ratio should a Facebook or Instagram video ad use?

In general, use 9:16 for full-screen vertical placements such as Stories and Reels, and 4:5 or 1:1 for feed placements. Check Meta’s current specifications in its own help pages before a campaign, because lengths and safe zones change. Videngine renders all of these ratios from the same source.

Is an AI marketing video generator the same as a video ad maker?

Not always. Some AI marketing video generators invent footage from a prompt, while a template-based video ad maker arranges your own photos, pages and data into a narrated ad. The first suits concept visuals; the second suits ads where accuracy and repeatability matter.

How many video ad variants should I make?

Make enough to answer one question at a time: two hooks, for example, in each format you actually run. Keep the offer and call to action fixed while you test the hook. If several elements change at once, extra variants add review time without adding clarity.

Can I use Videngine videos in paid ads?

Yes. Customers own the rendered files and scripts, can use them commercially and keep that right if they stop paying. You upload the MP4s to the ad platform yourself, because Videngine does not connect to ad accounts.
