Playlists can mirror the site's own categories, and each video can link back to the page it came from. YouTube channel for business turns this into a 30-day launch plan.

Shorts and long-form

Most businesses need both, cut from the same source: a vertical Short for reach and a landscape video for search and the website.

YouTube's help pages describe Shorts as square or vertical videos of up to three minutes, with wider videos treated as long-form. A Short needs a hook in its first seconds and burned-in captions; a long-form cut can carry the full specification or the whole explanation. Shorts vs long-form sets out which to start with by business type, and the Shorts maker guide covers structure and batching.

Publishing and automation

Uploads, metadata and pacing can be automated; approval, accuracy and replies to viewers still need a person.

A business channel is automated well when every video is reviewed before it is queued, the title and description are specific to that video, and uploads are paced rather than dumped in one go. YouTube automation for businesses separates what to automate from what to keep, and titles, descriptions and tags covers the fields field by field.

The rules that matter

Three sets of YouTube rules shape a business channel made with automation: disclosure, spam and repetitive content, and API limits.

  1. AI disclosure. YouTube asks creators to disclose realistic altered or synthetic content. What that means for a synthetic narrator, an AI-written script or generated footage is in YouTube AI disclosure.
  2. Spam and inauthentic content. YouTube's policies are aimed at mass-produced, near-identical videos. Videos built from different listings, products and articles, each with its own facts, are a different thing, but each one still needs to be worth watching on its own.
  3. API limits. Uploads through YouTube's API are counted against a daily allowance, so large channels publish in a steady flow.

Questions about YouTube for business

What is the best YouTube video creator for a business?

It depends on what the channel is made of. For filmed content, a camera and a timeline editor; for many videos made from listings, products, articles and help pages, a tool such as Videngine that renders them from the website and writes the metadata for each one.

Can I automate a YouTube channel for my business?

You can automate making the videos, writing the metadata and uploading them at a steady pace. Approval, accuracy and replies to viewers should stay with a person, and each video should be worth watching on its own rather than a near-copy of the last.

Should a business make Shorts or long-form videos?

Usually both, from the same source. A Short reaches people browsing the Shorts feed, and a landscape video works for search, playlists and the website.

Do I need to label AI narration on YouTube?

YouTube asks for disclosure of realistic altered or synthetic content. Its help page lists cases that need it and cases that don't; the YouTube AI disclosure guide sets out what it says for common business videos.

Yes. A channel Owner connects the channel once, and approved videos are published with their titles, descriptions and tags, at a pace within YouTube's daily upload allowance. YouTube publishing is part of the Scale plan and is rolling out.
