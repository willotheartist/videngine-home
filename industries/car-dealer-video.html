Car dealer video: a listing video for every vehicle in stock

The short answer

A car dealer video is a short narrated walkaround of one vehicle in stock, built from the photos and specification already on its listing: make, model, year, mileage, fuel, transmission, price and how to enquire. Because those exist for every unit, every vehicle can have one, not only the hero stock. Videngine reads the listing URL or your sitemap, drafts the script and renders a captioned 9:16 cut for social and a 16:9 cut for YouTube and your website.

Car dealer video for every vehicle, not a few hero videos

A video per stock unit beats a handful of showroom films, because buyers choose one specific vehicle and the material for its video already exists.

  • Stock turns over. Much automotive video marketing goes on a few hero cars, and a film of last month's best car advertises something you no longer have. A video tied to a stock number is made when the unit is listed and retired when it sells.
  • The photos and spec already exist. Your website holds the photos, specification and price for each unit. A used car video reuses them in walkaround order, with no second shoot and no editor.
  • Buyers shortlist remotely. A buyer comparing three hatchbacks, or a fleet manager comparing two minibuses far apart, decides from the advert which are worth a journey.
  • Two shapes from one listing. A 9:16 cut for short-form video on social, and a 16:9 cut for your website gallery and YouTube videos on your channel.

We will not quote a figure for how much video lifts enquiries; compare your own stock with and without it. Vehicles and equipment is one of the business types a Videngine workspace is set up for; see Videngine by industry for the others.

What a vehicle listing video should show

A vehicle listing video should walk round the vehicle in the order a buyer would on the forecourt, and state only facts already on the listing.

Photo order: front three-quarter, side profile, rear, interior, dashboard and odometer, boot or load area, engine bay, wheels and tyres, then any marks or damage, shown honestly. If a photo is missing, drop the scene rather than borrow one from another vehicle.

Pronunciation and an example 40-second scene plan

Set make, model and place names once in the brand's pronunciation lexicon, and every later video says them the same way.

A misread make undoes the rest of the video. Typical entries: Peugeot (PUR-zhoh) and Citroën (SIT-roh-en), including feeds that drop the accent; Volkswagen Touareg (TWAH-reg); codes such as GTI read as “G T I” and 2.0 TDI as “two-litre T D I”; branch towns such as Leicester (LESS-ter).

From stock list to video: the workflow

In Videngine one stock unit goes in and two cuts come out; your team's time goes on choosing units and reviewing drafts.

  1. Pick the stock. Have Videngine read your website's sitemap and pick vehicle listings, paste one listing URL, or send a feed from your stock system (see bulk video creation from a CSV). A headless browser reads each listing's text, photos and structured data, skipping navigation and cookie banners.
  2. Run a batch. Each listing becomes one video with a drafted script and timed scene plan to accept, edit or replace. Batches run unattended.
  3. Review with defect codes. Every video is reviewed in the studio. A rejection gets a code (pronunciation, audio, specification, photo, brand, render or mobile) that shows where the fix belongs.
  4. Render 9:16 and 16:9. MP4 (H.264) up to 1080p, captions burned in or as an .srt file. In the vertical cut a landscape photo sits over a blurred fill of itself, so the car is not cropped away.
  5. Put it where buyers look. Add the 16:9 cut to the vehicle's gallery on your website, publish to your connected YouTube channel with separate titles and tags for the Short and the long-form cut, and download the 9:16 cut for social. For third-party portals, check whether each accepts a video or a video link: Autotrader UK's help centre, for example, asks for a link to a public YouTube video and says it does not support YouTube Shorts, so link the landscape cut there.

Keeping vehicle videos honest as stock changes

A vehicle video describes one unit at one price on one day, so a price change, reservation or sale is a reason to re-render or retire it, not to edit it.

  • Price changes. Set a price policy per scene set: state the price and re-render when it changes, or leave it out and send viewers to the listing.
  • Re-render, do not edit. Update the listing and render again. The rebuild is saved as _v2 beside the original, so the ledger shows what was said and when; re-rendering the same video in the same month is free.
  • Reserved and sold. Decide whether a reserved unit's video stays up until the deal completes. Once sold, retire it from your website, YouTube and any portal link, so it stops drawing enquiries you cannot meet.

Advertising must not mislead, so keep narration to facts in the listing: no “one owner” or “full service history” the listing does not state, and no mileage rounded down. The brand's banned-phrases list blocks wording your compliance lead has ruled out. In the UK, the CAP Code says marketing communications must not materially mislead, and allows a VAT-exclusive price only where everyone the advert is clearly addressed to pays no VAT or can recover it, with the VAT stated prominently: check any van video that says “plus VAT”. In the US, the Federal Trade Commission says advertising must be truthful and non-deceptive and claims need evidence. Link to the full listing, and check your obligations with your compliance lead or trade body.

Dealer inventory video at group scale

At group scale the work is maintaining a few scene sets and a record, by stock number, of what was made, checked and published.

  • One scene set per vehicle type. Cars, vans, buses and coaches, trucks and plant each get their own facts card and scene order. Brand colours, typefaces and logo are set once; seats and brands are included, so each franchise keeps its own look.
  • Name files by stock number. For example ST48213_9x16_v1.mp4. When a van appears on your site, a portal and YouTube, the stock number finds every copy. Google Drive delivery adds a Sheet listing every vehicle with links to both cuts and the listing.
  • Keep the ledger. It records each version's review status, builder, reviewer and any defect code, and exports to CSV.
  • Separate building from approving. A workspace can require that the approver is not the builder, which suits groups where marketing builds and a site manager signs off.
  • Custom plan. From 1,500 videos a month: delivery straight into your listings, a scene set built for you, an agreed rollout, and optionally a coverage report of how much live stock has an approved video.

Questions dealers ask about vehicle listing video

How do I make a video for every car in stock?

Start from the listings you already publish, which carry photos, specification and price. Videngine reads your sitemap, turns each selected listing into a vertical and a landscape cut with narration and captions, and queues them for review.

What should a used car video include?

Make, model, variant, year, mileage, fuel, transmission, key specification, price policy, location and how to enquire. Show the exterior, interior, odometer, boot, engine bay, wheels and any marks or damage. Showing damage honestly saves a wasted journey.

How long should a vehicle listing video be?

Around 30 to 60 seconds suits most cars; a coach or specialist truck may need longer. In Videngine, a video up to two minutes counts as one video.

Can I add a dealer video to Autotrader or other car portals?

Each portal sets its own rules, so check its help pages first. Autotrader UK's help centre, for example, asks for a link to a public YouTube video and says it does not support YouTube Shorts, so publish the landscape cut to your channel and link that.

Does Videngine generate footage of the vehicle?

No. AI writes the script and the voice, but every picture is the listing's own photo, framed for the format with slow pan and zoom. It does not invent angles, features or footage, and has no presenters.
