Yacht broker video: a video programme for every boat on your books

Yacht broker video for every boat on your books: owner-approved listing videos for enquiry replies, viewings, price changes, boat show screens and every office.

The short answer

Yacht broker video works best as a programme rather than a film for a few flagships. Every boat on the books, on central agency or an open listing, gets a narrated, captioned video built from its own listing photos and specification, approved by the owner before release. The same video goes in the first reply to each enquiry, follows up viewings, loops silently on boat show screens, is re-rendered when the price changes and is retired when the boat is sold or withdrawn.

Yacht broker video for every boat on your books

A yacht broker video programme gives every boat on the books its own listing video, then reuses that video at each step of the sale.

What a single yacht for sale video shows, how builder and marina names are said, and the per-boat workflow are in the boat listing video guide. This page covers what the brokerage runs around it:

  • Every boat, not only the flagships. Filming tends to go to the larger yachts; a listing video costs the same for any boat.
  • Owner approval first. No video goes out until the owner has seen it.
  • A video in the first reply. Made on the day of instruction, it is ready when the first enquiry lands.
  • Boat show screens. The same cuts loop on the stand, captioned, with the sound off.
  • A monthly round-up. One video introducing the month's new instructions.
  • Price reduction updates. A re-render at the new price, sent to buyers who already asked.

On an open listing the same boat may sit with two or three brokers, each with its own photos, so build yours only from your own listing. Each boat's 9:16 and 16:9 cuts are named by your listing reference, so the file in the enquiry reply is the file on the stand screen. For charter, see charter yacht video.

A video in the first reply to an enquiry

The first reply decides whether the buyer calls, so build and approve the video at instruction, not on request. Put the link in your enquiry reply template beside the listing link: the boat's page on your website, or the landscape cut on YouTube. If your site is static HTML built from a GitHub repository, Videngine can publish the approved video into the boat's page with a player, transcript and captions. A developer can use the read-only API (Growth plan) to look up the approved video by stock number, here your listing reference. Videngine does not connect to your enquiry system.

Viewing follow-ups and price reductions

After a viewing, send the video again for the partner who was not aboard. For a price reduction, update the listing and re-render: the new version is saved as _v2 beside the original, and re-rendering the same video in the same month is free. Lead the script with the new asking price, keep the tax status as listed, and send the new cut to buyers who enquired at the old price (see the lead guide's price policy).

Listing services

Each listing service sets its own video rules, so check its broker help pages first. TheYachtMarket's own article, for example, says brokers add video by giving the URL of a video hosted on YouTube, Vimeo, DailyMotion or Wistia; it dates from 2020, so check the current form. For each other service, find out whether an advert takes an uploaded file, a pasted link or a video URL in your listing feed. Where only a link is taken, link the landscape cut on YouTube.

Owner approval and listing rights

Build a brokerage listing video only from photos you have the right to use, have the owner approve it before release, and retire it when the boat is sold or withdrawn.

Photos you have the right to use

Holding a copy of a photo is not the same as the right to use it. GOV.UK's copyright notice on digital images and photographs says the person who creates an image will generally be the first owner of the copyright, that a commissioned photographer usually keeps it, and that a user will usually need the copyright owner's permission. In practice:

  • Your photographer's shoot. Check the licence covers video, social and boat show use, not only the listing.
  • The owner's photos. Get written permission in the listing agreement.
  • Another broker's photos. On an open listing, they belong to that broker or its photographer. Do not lift them.
  • Brochure images. A builder's shot shows a sister ship, not this boat.

Videngine does not check who owns the pictures it is given; that stays with the brokerage.

The owner approves the video

  1. Build from approved particulars, once the owner has signed off the listing text.
  2. Review in the studio. A rejection gets a defect code (pronunciation, audio, specification, photo, brand, render or mobile), so the fix goes to the right person.
  3. Send the owner the draft. Send the landscape cut and the script, and ask them to check the price, tax status, location and anything said about the boat's history.
  4. Respect privacy requests. If the owner does not want the yacht's name or home berth made public, take them out of the script and drop photos showing the name on the transom.
  5. Record and release. Keep the owner's approval with the listing file, then approve the video in the studio and publish it.

Sold or withdrawn: retire it everywhere

Take the video down on the day the status changes: the boat's page, YouTube, every listing service carrying the link, the show playlist and any round-up still being sent. The API's publishing history shows where Videngine published it; anything uploaded by hand needs your own list. Keep the ledger entry in case anyone later asks what was advertised.

Boat show video and the monthly round-up

On a boat show stand, loop each boat's landscape cut with captions burned in and the sound off, because narration rarely carries across a busy hall or pontoon.

  • Burn the captions in, so the words show on whatever screen and player the stand uses.
  • Order the loop. Boats at the show first, then boats on the books that stayed at home.
  • Check prices the morning the show opens. Re-render any changed price and take out boats that went under offer.
  • Test on the real screen. Captions must read from the front of the stand; a portrait screen takes the 9:16 cut.
  • Show the listing reference on the closing card, so visitors can ask for the boat by reference.
  • Check the organiser's rules on stand screens and sound before you hire screens.

The monthly new-listings round-up

A round-up is one video introducing the month's new instructions, made from a page rather than one listing. Publish a "new this month" page with each boat's lead photo, headline facts and reference, then turn that page into a video (see URL to video). A video up to two minutes counts as one; at 12 to 15 seconds a boat, that fits about eight. With more, split by sail and power or by office. Check every boat is owner-approved and still available before it goes out.

  • A ledger by listing reference. Name files by reference, for example BR2041_16x9_v1.mp4 (illustrative), with a rebuild saved as _v2 beside it. The ledger records each version, builder, reviewer, status and defect code, and exports to CSV (Growth) for checking against each office's stock list.

  • Builder and approver apart. A workspace can require that the approver is not the builder: the broker builds, the sales manager approves. Reviewer sign-off and defect codes are listed on the Scale plan.
  • Delivery you own. Google Drive delivery (Growth, rolling out) puts both cuts in a folder the brokerage owns, with a sheet linking every boat to both cuts.

Questions yacht brokers ask about a video programme

What is a yacht broker video?

A short narrated video of one boat on a broker's books, built from the listing's photos and specification. A brokerage reuses it through the sale: enquiry replies, viewing follow-ups, boat show screens and price updates.

Does the owner need to approve a video of their yacht?

Make it your rule. Send the owner the draft cut and the script before release, and ask them to check the price, tax status, location and whether the boat's name may be shown. Keep their approval with the listing file.

Can I use photos from another broker's listing of the same boat?

Not without permission. On an open listing, photos on another broker's advert usually belong to that broker or its photographer. Build your video from your own photos, or get a written licence first.

What should play on a boat show stand screen?

Each boat's landscape cut on a loop, with captions burned in and the sound off. Start with the boats at the show, then the boats on your books that stayed at home, and check every price the morning the show opens.

Can each office have its own branded videos?

Yes. Each brand has its own logo, colours and voice rules: Starter includes one brand, Growth three and Scale ten. Keep one pronunciation list and copy it into every brand, so names sound the same in every office.
