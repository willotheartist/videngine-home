Ecommerce product video: a video for every product in your shop

The short answer

An ecommerce product video is a short narrated, captioned video of one product, built from what its product page already holds: photos, name, variant, key specification, dimensions and price. Because every product has those, every product can have one, starting with best sellers and the products buyers ask most about. Videngine reads your shop's sitemap or a CSV export of your catalogue, turns each product into a vertical 9:16 and a landscape 16:9 cut, and holds every video for review before it goes out.

Ecommerce product video for every product, not a few hero films

Every product a buyer might choose should have its own video, because the photos and facts it needs are already in your catalogue.

  • The material exists. Each product page holds photos, a title, the variant, the specification, dimensions and a price. A product video narrates them in order, with no second shoot and no editor.
  • Buyers decide on one product. Someone comparing two backpacks wants the capacity, the material and whether a laptop fits. A product page video answers that on a phone.
  • Hero films leave the long tail bare. A shoot budget covers a handful of lines, rarely the whole range.
  • One product, several shapes. A 9:16 cut for social and ads, a 16:9 cut for the product page and YouTube, and 1:1 or 4:5 for square galleries and feeds.

We will not quote a sales lift; compare products with and without video in your own analytics. Products and e-commerce is one of the business types a Videngine workspace is set up for; see Videngine by industry for the others.

From shop to video: a shop owner's workflow

One product goes in and two cuts come out; your time goes on choosing products, setting the rules once and reviewing drafts.

There are two ways in. The studio can read your shop's sitemap and list its product pages, and you pick which to turn into videos, one at a time or in batches. Or send a CSV export of the catalogue, one row per product or variant, with SKU, title, variant, price, image links and key specification (see bulk video creation from a CSV). Batch runs from a CSV or JSON file start on the Growth plan.

  1. Pick a pilot family. About ten products buyers judge the same way, such as all the bags, including one with look-alike variants.
  2. Set the brand once. Logo, colours and typefaces; a pronunciation lexicon for your brand, product lines and materials (bouclé, chambray, “XL” read as “extra large”); banned phrases such as “guaranteed to fit”; and a target length.
  3. Set the price and variant rules. Decide whether videos state the price, and which variant each one shows (see below).
  4. Bring in the products. Pick pages from the sitemap, or upload the CSV. Each product becomes one video, with a drafted script and scene plan to accept, edit or replace.
  5. Review every draft. Check the script against the product page, watch with and without sound, and check the vertical cut on a phone. A rejection gets a defect code: pronunciation, audio, specification, photo, brand, render or mobile. The approver can be required to differ from the builder.
  6. Deliver. Download the MP4s, named by SKU, or on Growth have them delivered to a Google Drive folder you own, with a sheet linking both cuts of every product (rolling out).
  7. Roll out by family. Once the pilot passes, run the next family. The video ledger records each version, builder, reviewer, status and defect code, and exports to CSV.

Variants: the colour, size and photo must agree

A product video shows one variant, so the variant named, the photo shown and the price stated must all belong to it.

  • Colours. Give each colour its own row, or its own URL if your shop has one, with only that colour's photos, and name it in the narration and on screen.
  • Sizes. Name the size range as listed. Where size changes the product, as with a rug or a desk, name the size shown and give its dimensions.
  • Look-alike variants. A separate video for each, with the variant name on screen in every scene, so a dove grey is never sold as a slate.
  • Prices and stock. State the price of the variant shown, or leave it out and send viewers to the product page. Leave stock levels out.

Illustrative example: a fictional canvas tote in olive and navy becomes two rows, TOTE-OLV and TOTE-NVY, and files such as TOTE-OLV_9x16_v1.mp4. A navy photo in the olive video is rejected with a photo defect.

What Videngine does not do

  • Product photography or filming. The pictures come from your photos, screenshots or your own footage. Weak photos make a weak video.
  • 3D or AR viewers. It renders flat MP4 files, not spins, 3D models or virtual try-on.
  • Generated footage of the product. AI writes the script and the voice; it does not invent footage, simulate the product in use or show a colour you have not photographed. There are no presenters, and narration is in English only.

Keeping product videos in step with the catalogue

A product video describes one product at one price on one day, so a change in the catalogue is a reason to re-render or retire the video, not to edit it.

  • Price changes. If a video states the price, re-render it when the price changes. A sale price needs an end date and an owner, so keep offers in ad cuts with a planned retire date.
  • Specification changes. New dimensions, a new material or different box contents mean a re-render. The rebuild is saved as _v2 beside the original, so the ledger shows what each version said and when.
  • Discontinued products. Retire the video wherever it was published: the gallery, marketplaces, YouTube and any ad still running, so it stops selling something you cannot ship.
  • Seasonal ranges. Render when the range goes live and retire the videos when it ends; a returning line gets a fresh render.

Re-rendering the same video in the same month is free, so a price change or a corrected pronunciation does not cost another video. To find what changed, compare this week's catalogue export with last week's on the price, title, variant and image columns. If your sitemap sets lastmod, which the sitemaps.org protocol defines as the date the page was last modified, it also shows which product pages changed.

Questions shop owners ask about product video

How do I make a video for every product in my shop?

Start from the product pages you already publish. Videngine reads your sitemap or a CSV export of your catalogue, turns each selected product into a vertical and a landscape cut with narration and captions, and queues every video for review before it goes out.

How long should an ecommerce product video be?

Long enough to show what a buyer checks, and no longer. In Videngine a video up to two minutes counts as one video. Marketplaces set their own limits: Etsy's help centre says listing videos may be 3 to 15 seconds and play without audio.

Will the video show the right colour and size?

It shows the variant you give it. Give each colour its own row or URL with that colour's photos, name the size shown where size changes the product, and reject any draft whose photo, name or price belongs to another variant.

Can I add the same product video to eBay?

Yes, if it fits eBay's rules: one video per listing, up to 150 MB and ideally a minute or less, uploaded as a file rather than a YouTube link.

Does Videngine film or generate footage of my products?

No. AI writes the script and the voice, but every picture comes from your own photos, screenshots or footage. It does not invent footage, build 3D or AR viewers, or use presenters.
