VDP video: putting a video on every vehicle detail page

VDP video for every vehicle detail page: where the player sits, poster-first loading for page speed, VideoObject markup, and what happens when stock sells.

The short answer

A VDP video is a short narrated video of one vehicle, played on its vehicle detail page. Put the landscape 16:9 cut first in the photo gallery or directly under it, with the lead photo as its poster, and load little more than that poster until the buyer presses play. Give the page VideoObject markup, a caption track and a transcript. Tie the video to the stock number, so a price change brings a re-render and a sale retires it.

Where a VDP video goes on the vehicle detail page

A VDP video goes first in the photo gallery, or directly under it, because that is where a buyer on a vehicle detail page is already looking.

A vehicle detail page (VDP) is the page a dealer website or marketplace gives one unit in stock: gallery, price, specification, finance, stock number and enquiry form. What the video should show is covered in car dealer video: a listing video for every vehicle in stock; this guide is about the page it plays on, for cars, vans and trucks, buses and coaches or machinery.

  • First item in the gallery. The buyer meets it while swiping through photos. This needs a gallery that accepts a video item; ask your website provider.
  • Directly under the gallery. The fallback where the gallery takes images only, above the specification and finance calculator.
  • Not in a tab or a pop-up. A “Video” tab is easy to miss, and a pop-up covers the photos.

Which cut, and which poster

Use the landscape 16:9 cut in the gallery on desktop and, usually, on phones, because the gallery is built around landscape photos. Test the vertical 9:16 cut where your site opens video full screen on phones.

Make the poster, the still shown before play, the VDP's lead photo. The gallery then opens on the same image whether or not the video has loaded. Without a poster, MDN's video element reference notes, nothing is displayed until the first frame is available.

Autoplay: click to play is the safer default

Show the player with controls and let the buyer press play. Chrome's autoplay policy says muted autoplay is always allowed, allows sound only in some cases, and advises never assuming a video will play. WebKit's iOS video policy lets muted video autoplay and plays video inline on iPhone only with the playsinline attribute. Muted autoplay loses the narration and starts the download whether or not anyone watches; if you use it, choose a cut with burned-in captions and add an unmute button.

VDP video and page speed: load nothing until play

A VDP video should cost the page almost nothing until a buyer presses play: the poster shows, little or no video data loads, and the player's space is held from the start.

Google's Core Web Vitals measure real-world page experience. web.dev sets the targets at the 75th percentile of page loads: Largest Contentful Paint (loading) within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint at 200 milliseconds or less, and Cumulative Layout Shift (visual stability) at 0.1 or less. Google Search Central highly recommends good Core Web Vitals for success with Search. A VDP video touches two.

  • Largest Contentful Paint. For a video, web.dev uses the poster's load time or the first frame's, whichever is earlier. At the top of a VDP the poster is often the largest thing in view, so compress it like the lead photo it replaces.
  • Cumulative Layout Shift. web.dev says always include width and height attributes on images and video elements, or reserve the space with CSS aspect-ratio, so the specification below does not jump when the player appears.

The preload attribute sets how much of the file to fetch before play. MDN describes none as no preloading, metadata as fetching only details such as length, and auto as allowing the whole file to download; autoplay overrides it. web.dev's guide to fast playback notes that preload is a hint the browser may ignore.

Markup, captions and transcript on a VDP

Each VDP with a video needs one VideoObject block describing that vehicle's video, a caption track in the player and the transcript as text on the page.

Google says VideoObject markup must be added to a page where users can watch the video, and requires name, thumbnailUrl and uploadDate. Google keeps most video features for watch pages and gives a product page with a 360 video as an example of a page that is not one; a VDP is in that group, but can still appear as a text result and a Google Images result with a video badge. VideoObject schema covers every property and testing.

PropertyWhat a VDP puts there
nameYear, make, model and variant; add the stock number if two units match
descriptionWhat the video shows: mileage, colour, the condition photos it covers
thumbnailUrlThe poster (the lead photo) at one stable URL
uploadDateWhen this video first went on the VDP, with a timezone
contentUrl or embedUrlcontentUrl for an MP4 file; embedUrl for a player link or embed

Captions are a Level A requirement in WCAG 2.2: success criterion 1.2.2 asks for captions for all prerecorded audio content in synchronised media. Add the WebVTT file as a track in the player and the transcript as text in the page's HTML, so a buyer on a train can follow without sound and crawlers that do not run scripts can read it (see video transcripts and captions). List each VDP with a video in a video sitemap, and remove the entry when the video comes down.

Questions for your website provider

  • Can the VDP gallery hold a video, and in which position?
  • Does it take an MP4 upload, a video URL per vehicle, or only an embed?
  • Can the player carry a WebVTT track, and the page a transcript?
  • Does the VDP template output VideoObject in the HTML the server sends?
  • What happens to the video and its markup when a vehicle is marked sold?

Keeping the VDP video right as stock changes

A VDP should only play an approved video that matches the vehicle's current price and status, so tie it to the stock number and decide in advance what a price change or sale does.

  • One stable link per stock number. With listing delivery on the Custom plan, each stock number keeps one link that plays only a cut that passed review, and switches to the new cut when a rebuild is approved. Nobody edits the VDP, and an unreviewed draft never reaches it.
  • A price change means a re-render. If the video states the price, update the listing and render again; the rebuild is saved as _v2 beside the original, free within the same month. Review price rebuilds the same day, or leave the price out of the video.
  • Sold units: retire or keep, per your policy. Write it down before launch.

Questions about VDP video

What is a VDP video?

A VDP video is a short video of one vehicle played on its vehicle detail page, the page a dealer website gives each unit in stock. Videngine builds it from that page's own photos and specification, with narration and captions, in a landscape and a vertical cut.

Where should the video go on a vehicle detail page?

First in the photo gallery, or directly under it if the gallery takes images only. Use the landscape cut there, with the lead photo as the poster, and keep it out of tabs and pop-ups.

Should a VDP video autoplay?

Click to play is the safer default. Chrome always allows muted autoplay but allows autoplay with sound only in some cases, and a muted video loses its narration. If you do autoplay muted, use burned-in captions and an unmute button.

Will a video slow down my vehicle detail pages?

Not much, if the page loads only the poster until play. Set preload to none or metadata, give the player width and height, and avoid autoplay, which starts the download whether or not anyone watches.

Does a VDP need VideoObject markup?

It should have it, on the page where the video plays, with name, thumbnailUrl and uploadDate. A VDP is not a watch page, so expect a text result or a Google Images result with a video badge rather than full video features.

What happens to the VDP video when the vehicle sells?

Whatever your sold policy says. Retiring it is the simple default: remove the video, its markup and its sitemap entry together.
