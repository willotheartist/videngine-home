The short answer

A boat listing video is a short narrated tour of one boat for sale, built from the listing's own photos and specification: builder and model, year, length, beam, draft, engines and hours, cabins, location, price and how to enquire. Because those already exist, every listing can have one. Videngine reads the listing URL, writes the script, says names from your pronunciation lexicon and renders a captioned vertical and landscape cut for review.

Why every boat for sale should have a video, not only the flagship yachts

Every boat on a broker's books can carry a video because the costly work, photographing the boat and writing up its specification, is already done. Filmed yacht video tends to be kept for the larger yachts, because a day at the berth costs much the same whatever the boat's size.

  • Brokers carry many listings. New boats arrive every week across several marinas. Hand editing does not keep up; starting from the listing page can.
  • Buyers shortlist remotely. The buyer is often abroad and narrows the field from listings before flying out to view. A narrated tour of the layout helps them decide which boats are worth the trip.
  • The photos and specification already exist. A boat for sale video reuses them, so there is no second shoot.
  • Two shapes from one listing. A vertical 9:16 cut for short-form video on Reels, Shorts and TikTok; a landscape 16:9 cut for your website and your YouTube channel.

We will not quote a figure for how much video lifts enquiries; compare your own listings with and without it. Boats are one of seven industries Videngine serves. Running a boat marketplace? See video for every listing on a marketplace.

What a boat listing video should show

A boat listing video should show the boat in the order a buyer walks through it on a viewing, and state only what the listing states.

That order is profile under way or at the berth, deck and cockpit, helm and electronics, saloon, galley, cabins, heads, engine room, then tender and toys.

From listing URL to boat listing video: the workflow

In Videngine, a listing URL or your sitemap goes in, and each boat comes out as a reviewed vertical and landscape video named by its listing reference.

  1. Start from the listing URL or the sitemap. Paste one listing, or let the studio read your website's sitemap and list every boat on it. A headless browser keeps each page's text, photos and structured data and drops navigation and cookie banners.
  2. Pick a batch. Choose the boats to turn into videos. Each listing becomes one video, batch jobs run unattended, and files are named by stock number or listing reference.
  3. Check the script and scene plan. Accept the draft narration and timed scene plan, edit a line or supply your own script, and strike anything the listing does not say.
  4. Review every video. Watch once with sound and once without; captions are transcribed from the finished audio, so they match the voice. A workspace can require that the person who approves a video is not the person who built it.
  5. Render 9:16 and 16:9. Each boat gets a vertical and a landscape MP4 (H.264), up to 1080p, with captions burned in or as an .srt file. A one-minute video typically renders in a few minutes.
  6. Send it where buyers look. Add the landscape cut to the boat's page on your website, or deliver both to a Google Drive folder you own, with a Google Sheet linking every boat to its files. Publish to your connected YouTube channel, with separate titles and descriptions for the Short and the long-form cut, and post the vertical cut on social. For listing portals, check whether each one accepts a video upload or a video link on the advert.

Yacht charter video needs its own template

A yacht charter video sells a week aboard rather than the boat itself, so it leads with different facts and asks for a different action.

ElementBoat for saleCharter yacht
Leads withBuilder, model, year, locationYacht name, cruising area, guests and cabins
Key factsLength, beam, draft, engines and hoursCabins and berths, crew, tender and water toys
PriceAsking price and VAT or tax status, as statedWeekly rate, season and terms, worded as the listing words them
Scenes that differEngine room, helm and electronicsDeck spaces, crew, water toys, cruising-area photos if supplied

Charter rates change by season. Quote the weekly rate exactly as the listing does, and re-render when it changes so last season's figure is not still circulating on YouTube.

Keeping boat videos accurate: price reductions, under offer, sold

A boat video describes one boat at one asking price, so treat every price or status change as a reason to re-render or retire it, not to patch it.

  • Set a price policy per template. Either state the price and re-render when it changes, or leave it out and send viewers to the listing for the current figure.
  • Price reductions. Update the listing, then render again. The rebuild is saved as _v2 beside the original, never over it, and re-rendering the same video in the same month is free.
  • Under offer. Decide in advance whether the video keeps running. Pause promotion, or re-render once the listing shows the new status.
  • Sold. Retire the video wherever it lives: the boat's page, YouTube and social accounts.

Questions brokers ask about boat listing videos

How do I make a boat listing video from the photos I already have?

Put the listing photos in walk-through order, from profile to engine room, and write narration that states only what the specification says. Add captions for people watching without sound. Videngine does this from the listing URL and renders a vertical and a landscape cut for review.

How long should a yacht broker video be?

Long enough to cover the key specification and main spaces, short enough to hold attention on a phone. Around 30 to 60 seconds suits most boats for sale. In Videngine, a video up to two minutes counts as one video.

Can I add a boat for sale video to listing portals?

It depends on the portal. Check each portal's own help pages for whether it takes an uploaded video, a link to a video hosted elsewhere such as YouTube, or neither. Your own website and YouTube channel are always open to you.

Can the narration be in other languages for international buyers?

Not today. Videngine narrates in English only, with English captions. If most of your buyers expect their own language, plan for that separately.

Does Videngine film or generate footage of the boat?

No. AI writes the script and the narration voice, but the pictures are the listing's own photos, framed for each format with slow pan and zoom. Videngine does not invent views, cabins or sailing footage, and it has no presenters.
