The short answer

Estate agent social media video works when each post carries listing news: just listed, price reduced, an open house, a weekly round-up of new instructions, and under offer or sold where the seller agrees. Each vertical cut opens on the strongest photo and the location, states the price as the listing does, carries burned-in captions and ends with one step to the full listing. Videngine renders these from your listings; you schedule them in your own social tool.

Which property videos work on social, and which do not

A property video earns a place on your feed when it carries listing news someone can act on this week: the home is new, cheaper, open to view or moving.

Building the listing video itself is covered in real estate listing video for every property; this page covers what to post and how to cut it.

What does not belong on the feed

Anything the listing does not support stays out.

  • Claims beyond the particulars. A view, a school catchment or a walking time the listing does not state.
  • Who the home would suit. Describe the rooms, size and location, never the buyer.
  • Posts the market has overtaken. A just listed Reel for a home now under offer or withdrawn.
  • Revealing photos. Family pictures, a car registration, or signs the home is empty.

Property advertising rules that apply to social posts

The rules for your listings also govern your posts: the CAP Code in the UK, fair housing law in the US.

UK: the CAP Code and material information

  • Your accounts are in scope. The ASA says the CAP Code has applied since 2011 to non-paid-for space online under the advertiser's control, naming a brand's Facebook page, Instagram profile and YouTube channel, and any other social media channels. In June 2026 it ruled against an estate agent's leaflet and Facebook post that compared its service with named competitors.
  • Keep to the particulars. CAP Code section 3 says marketing must not materially mislead (rule 3.1) or omit material information (rule 3.3). The ASA's advice to estate agents cites a “private drive” that was shared.
  • Material information. The National Trading Standards Estate and Letting Agency Team published guidance on Part A material information for property listings in 2022; Part A includes the price or rent, the council tax band and, for sales, tenure. The announcement does not mention video, so put those facts in the post caption and link to the full listing. The team's material information page now points to the government's June 2026 plans for homebuying sales packs, so check it for changes.
  • Price reductions. CAP Code rule 3.39 says price comparisons must not mislead by falsely claiming a price advantage. Show the old figure only if it was the genuine asking price; otherwise say “new price”.
  • Under offer and sold. The ASA's privacy advice says marketers should take care not to feature recognisable property of members of the public without permission, and its advice on sales claims warns agents to be careful with the definition of “sold”. So wait for the seller's agreement.

Lettings carry their own rent wording rules; see rental listing video. For plot releases, see new development video.

US: fair housing advertising

HUD states that discriminatory housing advertisements are illegal under the Fair Housing Act, including adverts indicating a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, colour, religion, sex, disability, familial status or national origin; its examples are “no children” and “no wheelchairs”. On a realtor's TikTok, describe the home, never the household you picture in it, and set that rule as banned phrases so every script follows it. Take specific questions to your broker.

Platform labels for property posts

Each platform asks two questions of a property post, whether it is commercial and whether it contains realistic AI content, so answer both once per series.

Checked on each platform's own pages on 16 October 2026; “our reading” is ours, not the platform's. Labels change, so check current guidance.

From listing to scheduled post

  1. Pick the listings from your sitemap, or paste one listing URL; on Growth, load a CSV from your property software (see bulk video creation from a CSV).
  2. Set each series once: scene set, status line, price policy and banned phrases.
  3. Render the 9:16 cut, plus 4:5 or 16:9 if wanted, up to 1080p, named by listing reference, such as LR30517_9x16_v1.mp4 (illustrative).
  4. Review against the listing on the day. A rejection gets a defect code, such as specification for a wrong price; a workspace can require a different approver, such as the branch manager.
  5. Schedule in your social tool, with the price and, in the UK, the council tax band and tenure in the caption, and the listing link where the platform allows.
  6. Rebuild or retire. Render again when a price or status changes; take posts down when a home leaves the market.

Questions estate agents ask about social media video

What videos should an estate agent post on social media?

Listing news: just listed, price reduced, open house, a weekly round-up of new instructions, and under offer or sold where the seller agrees. Film the team and the area separately.

Should estate agents show the price in property Reels?

Decide per series. Show the asking price and its qualifier on just listed, price reduced, open house and round-up posts. Leave it off under offer and sold posts unless the seller agrees.

Can I post an under offer or sold video?

Only with the seller's agreement. The ASA advises marketers to take care not to feature recognisable property of members of the public without permission. Use the status the listing shows and leave the agreed price out.

Does the CAP Code apply to an estate agent's Instagram and TikTok?

Yes. The ASA says the CAP Code covers non-paid-for space online under the advertiser's control, naming a brand's Instagram profile and any other social media channels. Keep every claim to the listing.

Can Videngine post to Instagram and TikTok?

No. Videngine delivers MP4 files by download or to your Google Drive folder (Growth, rolling out), and publishes to YouTube on Scale (rolling out). Schedule Reels and TikToks in your own social tool.

Do AI-narrated property videos need an AI label?

Check each platform. Meta's rule covers realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, which can include a synthetic narrator. TikTok and YouTube ask for realistic synthetic content to be disclosed. If unsure, label.
