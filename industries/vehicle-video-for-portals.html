Vehicle listing video for portals: getting a video onto every advert

How to get a vehicle listing video onto Auto Trader and other portals: YouTube links, video URLs in your stock feed, which cut to send, and price changes.

The short answer

Most portals take video in one of three ways: a file uploaded to the advert, a link pasted onto it (usually a public YouTube video), or a video URL carried in your stock feed against each stock number. Auto Trader UK, for example, takes a public YouTube link and does not support Shorts, so send the landscape cut. Build and approve each video first, keep one link per stock number, and re-render or retire the video when the price or status changes.

How portals take video: an upload, a YouTube link or a feed field

Portals take video in one of three ways: a file you upload to the advert, a link you paste onto it, or a video URL that your stock feed or a media provider sets on the stock record.

For any other portal, check three things before you build: that your package includes video, whether it takes a file or a link, and which video hosts it displays.

Video URLs in your stock feed

In a feed, video is one more field on the stock record, keyed by stock number, so the URL in that field goes to every portal the feed serves and nobody pastes links by hand.

Whoever manages your feed needs four things settled before the first video goes out.

  • The stock number is the join. Your stock system, the feed and Videngine should use the same reference. Videngine names files by stock number or reference, so ST50417_16x9_v1.mp4 (an illustrative stock number) lines up with that unit's feed row.
  • One owner per field. Decide whether the stock system, the feed provider or a media provider writes the video field, and keep everyone else out. Auto Trader UK's media updates guide notes that overwriting content is a common problem when more than one media management provider is in place. Cars.com's help article says manual edits lock a listing's fields, so the inventory feed stops updating them until someone releases the lock.
  • Not every feed has a video field. Google's vehicle listings feed specification, for example, lists image links but no video attribute. Ask your feed provider for its field list before you plan a feed route.
  • A YouTube link changes with every rebuild. YouTube says you can't replace a video, since any new upload gets a new URL. A corrected or repriced video therefore means a new link in the feed.

One stable link per stock number (Custom plan)

On the Custom plan, from 1,500 videos a month, listing delivery can be one stable link per stock number. It only plays a cut that has passed review, and it switches to the new cut when a rebuild is approved. Put it in the feed once and the feed never needs editing for that unit again: a price change becomes a rebuild and an approval, not a feed change.

A stable link helps only where the portal displays links from your video host. Auto Trader UK shows YouTube, Vimeo or approved partner domains only, so there the route stays a public YouTube link.

From Growth, the API lets your feed provider look up the approved video for a stock number and list its versions, to check the feed carries the current cut. See what to check in a video automation API.

Burn the captions in. They are part of the picture, so they show whatever the portal's player does with caption files, and carry the narration for a buyer scrolling with the sound off. Keep the .srt file for players that take a caption track. See also YouTube Shorts vs long-form videos.

Check each portal's content rules before you batch. A portal that bars contact details or branding in video needs those scenes left out of its cut.

Price changes, reserved and sold units on portals

The advert and its video must say the same thing, so when the feed changes a price or status on a portal, the video on that advert has to be re-rendered or retired too.

The lead guide explains why to re-render rather than edit, and how a rebuild is saved as _v2 beside the original; see keeping vehicle videos honest as stock changes. On portals, the extra work is the link.

On YouTube, a private video can only be seen by you and whomever you choose, and a deleted video is gone for good, so set sold units to private to keep a record. Three habits help:

  • Leave the price out if prices move often. The advert shows the live price beside the video, so a price change needs no rebuild.
  • Reconcile weekly. Join the ledger's CSV export to your feed export by stock number. Look for live adverts with no approved video, videos older than the last price change, and sold units whose video is still public.
  • Check the advert after a change. Auto Trader UK says a saved video will appear online within 30 minutes.

From stock to portal: the workflow

Every portal video follows the same steps in Videngine; only the delivery route differs by portal.

  1. Choose the units. Pick vehicle listings from your website's sitemap, paste one listing URL, or send a CSV or JSON export from your stock system. Batch runs from a file start on Growth; see bulk video creation from a CSV.
  2. Build. Each unit gets a 16:9 and a 9:16 cut up to 1080p, with captions burned in, and files named by stock number.
  3. Review. Check the cut against the portal advert the buyer will see. A rejection gets a defect code (pronunciation, audio, specification, photo, brand, render or mobile).
  4. Approve. A workspace can require the approver to be someone other than the builder. The ledger records each version, builder, reviewer, status and defect code.
  5. Deliver by one route per portal.
    • Upload: download the MP4, or use Google Drive delivery (Growth, rolling out), whose sheet lists every unit with links to both cuts.
    • YouTube link: publish the 16:9 cut as a public video, by hand or through YouTube publishing (Scale, rolling out), which writes the title, description, hashtags and tags. Paste the link onto the advert or into the feed's video field.
    • Stable link in the feed (Custom): set it once per stock number; each approved rebuild switches it.
  6. Check the advert. Open it on the portal and play the video, on a phone as well as a desktop.

Videngine does not log in to portals or post to them. Your team, your stock management system or your feed provider places the file or link, unless direct delivery to a portal is agreed as part of a Custom rollout.

Questions about video on portal adverts

How do I add a video to an Auto Trader listing?

Auto Trader UK's help centre says to upload the video to YouTube as a public video, then paste the link on the vehicle's images and video tab in Portal. It does not support YouTube Shorts, so link the landscape cut. Video is not included in the Franchise Starter package, and a saved video appears online within 30 minutes.

Can a video URL go in my stock feed?

Often, if your feed or the portal's API has a video field. The URL then travels with the stock record, keyed by stock number, to every portal the feed serves. Check which hosts each portal displays: Auto Trader UK shows YouTube, Vimeo or approved partner domains only.

Can I add video to CarGurus or Cars.com listings?

Ask your account manager or feed provider whether a video link in your feed is shown on the advert. We have not found a dealer help page from either portal that describes listing video, so we do not say either way.

What happens to a portal video when the price changes?

Re-render the video, approve it and replace the link. On YouTube a new upload always gets a new URL, so the advert or feed needs the new link. On the Custom plan, a stable link per stock number switches to the new cut when the rebuild is approved. Or leave the price out of the video.

Does Videngine upload videos to Auto Trader for me?

No. Videngine delivers MP4 files, a Google Drive folder and YouTube publishing (both rolling out), and an API. Your team, stock system or feed provider puts the file or link on the advert, unless direct delivery is agreed as part of a Custom rollout.

Should portal adverts get the vertical or landscape cut?

The landscape 16:9 cut, unless a portal's own specification asks for vertical. A vertical upload to YouTube of up to three minutes becomes a Short, which Auto Trader UK does not support. Keep the 9:16 cut for social and short-form.
