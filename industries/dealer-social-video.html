Car dealer social media video: Reels, Shorts and TikToks from your stock

The short answer

Car dealer social media video works best when it comes straight from stock: a vertical video for each unit that has just arrived or dropped in price, a weekly round-up of five units, a model series and ex-fleet batches. Each cut needs a hook in the first seconds, burned-in captions, a decided price policy, the stock number and branch, and a pointer to the listing. Videngine renders these from your listings; you post them to Instagram and TikTok through your own social tool.

Which stock videos work on social media, and which do not

Stock videos work on social when they carry news a follower can act on this week: a unit has arrived, its price has dropped, or a set of units suits one kind of buyer.

Building the listing video itself is covered in car dealer video for every vehicle in stock; this page covers which videos to post and how to cut them for a feed.

What not to post

  • Sold or reserved units. They draw messages you have to turn away. Take the post down when the deal is done.
  • Every unit, every day. Post the news; the website carries the full stock list.
  • Units in preparation. Pre-valet photos make a weak video.
  • Prices under review. If the price may move before the weekend, post without it or wait.

Why a forecourt film still has its place

A filmed forecourt video still earns its slot, because buyers judge the people and the site as well as the stock. A salesperson walking round a car or the team on a Saturday needs real footage, which Videngine does not create. Plan one filmed post a week and let stock videos carry the volume.

Every 9:16 stock video needs five things: a hook in the first two seconds, captions for sound off, a price policy, the stock number and branch, and one next step to the listing.

Platform labels and advertising rules for dealer posts

Each platform asks two label questions of a stock post, commercial content and AI content, and in the UK the CAP Code applies as it does to your website.

Commercial and AI labels by platform

Checked on each platform's own pages on 16 October 2026. Text marked “our reading” is ours, not the platform's.

PlatformCommercial contentAI content
Instagram and FacebookInstagram's help centre says organic branded content must use the paid partnership label to tag the brand. Our reading: it applies when a manufacturer or paid creator is involved, not to your own stock alone.Meta says it will require its AI disclosure tool on organic posts with “photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered”, and may apply penalties. Our reading: a synthetic narrator can count, so label if unsure.
TikTokTikTok's business help centre says you must turn on the commercial content disclosure setting when a post promotes a brand, product or service; choose “Your brand” for your own stock.TikTok's Community Guidelines say synthetic media showing realistic scenes must be clearly disclosed, and bar synthetic visual or audio likeness of an adult private figure. Our reading: use a house voice there, not a cloned staff voice.
YouTubeYouTube's paid promotion setting covers “branded content, sponsorships, endorsements, or other commercial relationships”. Our reading: tick it if a partner paid towards the video.See our YouTube AI disclosure guide: a synthetic narrator is not listed, so select Yes if unsure.

Length rarely limits a stock video: YouTube Shorts can run up to three minutes, Instagram says reels over three minutes won't be recommended to new audiences, and TikTok's developer guide says all creators can post three-minute videos. Labels and limits change, so check current guidance and decide once per series. For boosted posts, see video ads.

Advertising standards in the UK

The CAP Code covers your social accounts, not only paid ads. The ASA's page on its online remit says the Code has applied to non-paid-for space online since 2011, naming a brand's Facebook page, Instagram profile and YouTube channel as examples.

  • Keep claims to the listing. The car dealer video page covers misleading claims and VAT-exclusive prices; add any phrase your compliance lead rules out to the banned-phrases list.
  • Price drops need a real previous price. The ASA's guidance on promotional savings claims says a reference price must be a genuine established usual selling price, should have been charged for longer than the promotional one, and expects at least three months of pricing history. A 2023 ruling on a used car on a dealer's website found a “was price” had not been established as a reference. Unless the history is checked, show only the current price and ban “was”, “save” and “reduced”.
  • Staff accounts. CAP Code rule 2.3 says marketing must not falsely claim or imply the marketer is acting as a consumer, so staff sharing stock videos from personal accounts should say they work for you.

Outside the UK, check your local rules; take specific claims to your compliance lead.

From stock list to scheduled post: the workflow

Videngine makes and reviews the videos; your social tool schedules and posts them, because Videngine does not post to Instagram, TikTok or Facebook.

  1. Pick the units. Have the studio read your sitemap and pick Monday's new listings, or load a CSV from your stock system for Wednesday's price changes (see bulk video creation from a CSV).
  2. Set each series once. Give each series its own scene set, price policy, target length and banned phrases.
  3. Run the batch. Each unit gets a 9:16 cut and a 16:9 cut, with 1:1 or 4:5 if wanted, up to 1080p, named by stock number, such as ST20417_9x16_v1.mp4 (illustrative).
  4. Review on the day. Check each video against the stock system. A rejection gets a defect code, such as specification for a wrong price or pronunciation for a misread make. A workspace can require the approver to be someone other than the builder, such as a sales manager; pricing lists reviewer sign-off and defect codes on Scale.
  5. Deliver. Download the MP4s, or receive them in your Google Drive folder with a sheet linking both cuts per unit (Growth, rolling out). Shorts and 16:9 cuts can publish to your YouTube channel with titles, descriptions, hashtags and tags per cut (Scale, rolling out).
  6. Schedule in your social tool. Upload the 9:16 cut to Instagram, TikTok and Facebook, write each caption, and set the labels decided for the series.
  7. Retire and rebuild. When a unit sells, take its posts down. When a price changes, render again: the rebuild is saved as _v2, free within the same month, and the ledger records each version.

Questions dealers ask about social media video

What videos should a car dealer post on social media?

Stock videos with news in them: just-arrived units, price drops, a weekly round-up of five units, a model series and ex-fleet batches. Add one filmed forecourt post a week.

How often should a car dealer post on Instagram and TikTok?

As often as you can review properly; there is no single right number. The illustrative plan on this page gives each weekday one kind of stock video, so review fits a daily slot.

Should a dealer show prices in Reels and TikToks?

Decide per series. Show the price where it is the news, such as just-arrived units and price drops, and leave it out of posts that stay up for months. In the UK, a “was” price needs a genuine price history under ASA guidance.

Can Videngine post to Instagram and TikTok?

No. Videngine publishes to YouTube (Scale, rolling out) and delivers MP4 files by download or to your Google Drive folder. Post to Instagram, TikTok and Facebook from the apps or your social tool.

Do AI-narrated stock videos need an AI label?

Check each platform. Meta says it requires disclosure of realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created, which can include a synthetic narrator. YouTube's page does not list a synthetic narrator over real photos, so disclose if unsure. TikTok requires disclosure of synthetic media showing realistic scenes.

Does the CAP Code apply to a dealer's social media posts?

Yes. The ASA says the CAP Code covers non-paid-for space online that a business controls, naming a brand's Facebook page, Instagram profile and YouTube channel. Keep claims to the facts on the listing.
