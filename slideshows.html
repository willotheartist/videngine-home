The short answer

An AI slideshow maker turns a set of photos into a finished video: it orders the pictures, times each one, adds motion, writes and records narration, captions it and exports the formats each screen needs. For one personal slideshow, a free phone or design app is enough. What an AI slideshow maker does

A slideshow video maker does six jobs, and the AI part is usually the words, not the pictures.

  1. Choose and order the photos, from a wide opening shot to the details.
  2. Time each photo, ideally to a sentence of narration rather than a fixed beat.
  3. Add motion, usually a slow zoom or pan so a still doesn't look frozen.
  4. Write and record narration that says what the photos can't: the price, the specification, the reason to care.
  5. Caption it, so the video still works with the sound off.
  6. Export each format: 16:9 for a website or YouTube, 9:16 for Reels, Shorts and TikTok, and 1:1 or 4:5 for feeds.

An AI slideshow maker is not the same as an AI image-to-video generator, which animates one still photo into a short clip of motion that was never filmed. The two are compared in image to video AI vs a photo slideshow.

How Videngine makes slideshows

Videngine makes narrated slideshows from a page, a listing or a row of data, using that source's own photos.

It reads the photos from the page, or from photo URLs in a CSV or JSON row, and asks for the original when a site serves resized copies. Photos smaller than a 1080p frame needs are enlarged with a Lanczos filter and a light sharpen, never more than three times, and each photo is graded so the studio can flag the soft ones. AI writes the script from the page's facts and records the narration in a house voice, another synthetic voice or your own uploaded audio. Each photo gets a slow zoom and a short fade in; a photo that doesn't fit the frame sits over a blurred fill of itself, so a landscape photo works in 9:16 without losing the subject. Captions are transcribed from the finished audio, and an optional quiet music bed can sit under the voice.

One source usually gives a 9:16 and a 16:9 cut, up to 1080p, and batch runs make one video per row or page. Every video is reviewed in the studio, then downloaded, delivered to Google Drive, published to YouTube or published into the page it was made from with its markup. When Videngine is the wrong tool

Videngine is built for many narrated business videos, so a few jobs belong elsewhere.

  • A birthday, wedding or holiday slideshow set to a song: a phone's photo app, iMovie, Google Photos or Canva does it free.
  • Beat-synced edits, collages and grids: a timeline editor or a design tool.
  • Motion that was never filmed: an AI image-to-video generator; see the best AI video generators.
  • Narration in languages other than English: Videngine narrates in English only.

Questions about slideshow makers

What is the best AI slideshow maker?

It depends on the job. For one personal slideshow, a free phone or design app is enough; for a designed video edited by hand, a template tool fits; for a narrated video of every listing, product or page, an automated slideshow maker such as Videngine fits.

Can I make a slideshow video with music and narration?

Yes. For a business video, music usually works best as a quiet bed under a voiceover that carries the facts. Videngine adds an optional music bed under its narration; it does not make music-only slideshows set to a song.

How many photos do I need for a slideshow video?

As a rule of thumb, a 30-second slideshow uses six to nine photos and a 60-second one around twelve to sixteen, depending on how long the narration spends on each. Sharp photos matter more than many photos.

Can a slideshow work as a vertical video for Reels, Shorts and TikTok?

Yes, if landscape photos are framed for 9:16. Cropping can cut off the subject, so many tools place the photo over a blurred fill of itself instead, which is what Videngine does.

Is a slideshow the same as AI image to video?

No. A slideshow arranges real photos into a video, with motion added to each still. AI image-to-video invents new motion from one photo, which can look striking but may show things that are not really there.
