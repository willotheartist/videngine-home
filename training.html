Training videos from your documents: an AI training video maker

The short answer

An AI training video maker works best from documents a team already keeps accurate: procedures, onboarding pages, lesson notes and approved leaflets. Each document becomes a short narrated, captioned video with one objective, reviewed before release and re-rendered when the document changes. Videngine does this as MP4 files for an LMS, an intranet, YouTube or a website. It doesn't make avatar presenters, quizzes or SCORM packages, so for those an avatar training platform fits better.

Choosing an AI training video generator

AI training video tools fall into three groups: avatar presenters, screen recordings tidied with AI, and motion graphics built from documents. Which one fits depends on what the viewer has to learn and how often the material changes.

Kind of toolWhat it makesBest useLimits
Avatar presenterA synthetic presenter reads your script beside slidesWelcome messages, compliance briefings, material needed in several languagesNot a real colleague; cannot demonstrate a physical task
Screen recording with AI editsSoftware use recorded on screen, often with AI that trims pauses or adds captionsSoftware interfaces: menus, fields, clicksRe-record when the interface changes; slow to repeat across many procedures
Motion graphics from documentsNarrated scenes built from your text, with screenshots, photos or your own footageProcedures and policies already written down; large libraries kept in step with their documentsNo presenter; shows only what you have pictures of; only as clear as the document

Many teams use more than one: a presenter for the welcome message, narrated document-to-video for procedures that change. Videngine is the third kind. It fits when the material is procedural, there is a lot of it, and keeping each video in step with its document matters more than a face on screen. For presenter-led or multilingual courses, the avatar platforms are compared in Videngine vs Synthesia and Videngine vs Colossyan.

What to compare between AI training video makers

Ask every tool the same six questions. The first narrows the field fastest.

  1. Does the video itself need SCORM, quizzes, a presenter or other languages? If a compliance course must report scores to the LMS, plain MP4 files are the wrong fit. Videngine does none of these; see when Videngine is the wrong tool.
  2. What does it start from? A tool that starts from the maintained document keeps one master copy. A timeline-based editor makes a second copy that someone has to keep in step.
  3. How much control is there over the words? Check that approved wording can be narrated word for word, and that system names, part numbers and site names can be set once for every video.
  4. How are captions made and checked? Captions should follow the narration exactly and be easy to check before release.
  5. Who approves, and where? The owner of the procedure needs to see the script and the finished video before anything goes out.
  6. What does a change cost? Count the steps to correct one line after the procedure changes: re-record, re-edit or re-render.

What good training video needs

Good training video is short, has one objective, is captioned, and has been approved by the person responsible for the content.

  1. One objective per video, stated in the first sentence.
  2. Short: a few minutes rather than a recorded hour.
  3. Captioned, with a transcript, so it works with the sound off and for people who can't hear it.
  4. Approved by the owner of the procedure, course or leaflet before release.
  5. Versioned, so everyone knows which video matches which revision of the document.

“Understand the returns policy” is a topic. “Log a damaged-goods return in the warehouse system” is an objective, and it tells you which part of the document to use. A Videngine video is one render of up to two minutes, so a longer subject becomes a series of short videos.

Review and approval

Training states how work must be done, so a named owner signs off each video twice: once as a script and once as a finished video.

  1. Approve the script. Check the AI-written script and scene plan against the source section before rendering. Where wording is controlled, paste the approved text and Videngine narrates it word for word.
  2. Watch the render. The procedure owner watches the finished video in the studio against the document, once with sound and once without.
  3. Check the captions. Videngine transcribes captions back from the finished narration, so the words and timing follow the voice. Names, codes and numbers still need checking against the document.
  4. Release only what is approved. Nothing goes to the LMS, the Drive folder, YouTube or the website until it has been signed off.

The W3C's captions guidance notes that WCAG requires captions at Level A, and says automatic captions “do not meet user needs or accessibility requirements unless they are confirmed to be fully accurate.” A batch made from a CSV can be rendered in one go and still reviewed one video at a time.

Keeping videos current

When a procedure, policy or leaflet changes, the video should be re-rendered from the new version rather than patched. A training video that contradicts the current procedure is worse than none, because it looks authoritative.

Videngine saves a rebuild as a new version beside the original, so the ledger shows which video is current, and re-rendering the same video in the same month is free. A version number and date on the closing card ties the video to its document. Keeping the old version on record also shows which version staff were trained on.

Short videos are cheaper to keep correct: when one step changes, you rebuild one clip, not a long module. Four habits keep a library in step:

  • Link each video to its source: document, section and revision.
  • Give each document an owner who approves both the change and the rebuilt video.
  • Put rebuilds on the change checklist so affected videos are replaced before the change takes effect.
  • Retire, don't delete: replace the old file wherever it is published and keep a copy for your records.

Delivery

Training videos are delivered as MP4 files, which most learning platforms, intranets and video hosts accept.

Videngine's files can be downloaded, put in a Google Drive folder you own, published to YouTube as unlisted or private, or published into a page of a static website with a transcript and caption track. If your learning platform needs SCORM packages, quizzes or completion tracking, those come from the platform or an avatar training tool, not from Videngine.

The learning platform keeps the assessment and the completion record; the video is a lesson asset. Upload the .srt caption file with the MP4 where the LMS accepts one. Publishing into a page works for static HTML sites built from a GitHub repository. Each source typically gives a landscape 16:9 cut and a vertical 9:16 cut for staff who train on their phones.

When Videngine is the wrong tool

Some training needs things Videngine doesn't do.

  • A presenter on screen, many languages, quizzes or SCORM: an avatar training platform; see Videngine vs Colossyan.
  • Hands-on physical tasks: film a real demonstration.
  • Live software click-throughs: a screen recorder.
  • Narration in other languages: Videngine narrates in English only.

Questions about training videos

What is the best AI training video generator?

It depends on what the training needs. Avatar platforms suit training that needs a presenter, many languages or quizzes; screen recorders suit software walkthroughs; a document-to-video engine such as Videngine suits many short, narrated videos made from procedures, pages and notes you already maintain.

Can I turn a PDF or document into a training video?

Yes. Paste the document's text or give Videngine the page it lives on, and it writes a script you can edit or replace word for word, then renders a narrated, captioned video for review. It does not take PDF or Word files directly, so paste the text from them.

Can Videngine export SCORM?

No. Videngine delivers MP4 or WebM files, which most learning platforms accept as video; SCORM packages, quizzes and completion tracking come from the learning platform or an interactive training tool.

How do I keep training videos up to date?

Re-render the video from the revised document rather than editing the old file. Videngine keeps each rebuild as a new version, so you can see which video matches which revision.

How long should a training video be?

Long enough to cover one objective and no longer. Short videos are easier to find, rewatch and correct. A Videngine video is one render of up to two minutes, so longer topics are split into a series.

Are training videos accessible without captions?

Captions are needed for people who can't hear the audio and for anyone watching with the sound off. Videngine captions every video from its finished audio, burned in or as a separate file.
